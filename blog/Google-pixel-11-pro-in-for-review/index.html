Google Pixel 11 Pro In for Review

Review Team August 31, 2026 7 min read
GooglePixelHands-OnPhone Reviews

Our Google Pixel 11 Pro review unit just arrived, and we're kicking off testing before publishing a full verdict. Here's a quick look at what's in the box, our very first impressions, and exactly what we'll be putting through its paces over the coming days.

The Pixel 11 Pro launched alongside the rest of Google's Pixel 11 lineup on August 20, 2026, built around the new Tensor G6 chipset and a heavier focus on Gemini-powered AI features than any Pixel before it. Rather than rush out a snap judgment, we're spending real time with the phone across everyday use, camera comparisons, and battery testing before committing to a score. This post is a placeholder for that process: what we've unboxed, what stands out immediately, and what to expect when the full review goes live.

What's in the Box

Consistent with recent Pixel launches, the retail box includes the phone itself, a USB-C to USB-C cable, basic documentation, and a SIM ejector tool. There's no charging brick included, so if you don't already own a compatible USB-C PD charger or a Qi2 wireless charging pad, budget for one separately before your phone arrives.

First Impressions

Out of the box, the Pixel 11 Pro's 6.3-inch LTPO OLED display is the first thing that stands out, particularly its ability to drop its refresh rate as low as 1Hz for static content while still hitting a smooth 120Hz when scrolling or gaming. Google's claimed 3600-nit peak brightness figure will need real outdoor testing to confirm, but early indoor use shows rich, accurate color that matches what we've come to expect from Pixel's Pro-tier displays.

The redesigned camera bar carries over Google's now-familiar visual language, and the phone feels dense but well balanced in hand, helped by its aluminum frame and glass back. Software-wise, Android 16 with Google's latest Pixel-exclusive features feels immediately snappier than the outgoing Pixel 10 Pro, though we'll reserve judgment on how much of that is the new Tensor G6 chip versus simple day-one optimization.

What We're Testing Before the Full Review

  • Camera performance: Side-by-side daylight, low-light, and zoom comparisons against last year's Pixel 10 Pro and current flagship rivals, along with hands-on time with Camera Looks and Magic Capture.
  • Battery life: Real screen-on time across a full charge cycle, plus how quickly Qi2.2 wireless charging actually refills the battery compared to Google's stated figures.
  • Tensor G6 performance: Benchmark testing alongside real-world use, including sustained gaming sessions to check for thermal throttling.
  • Gemini AI features: How useful the phone's on-device Gemini integration actually is day to day, beyond the launch demo.
  • Display brightness in direct sunlight: Verifying Google's peak brightness claims against outdoor visibility in practice.
Editor's Note First impressions are just that — impressions. We deliberately hold off on scoring a phone until we've lived with it through a full charge-and-recharge cycle and shot in a range of real lighting conditions, not just a controlled first-day setup.

Our full Google Pixel 11 Pro review will follow once testing wraps, with a complete breakdown of display, camera, performance, battery, and software, along with a final score and buying recommendation.
