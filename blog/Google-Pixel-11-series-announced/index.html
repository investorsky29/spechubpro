Google Pixel 11 Series Review: Tensor G6, Cameras and Which Model to Buy

Global News Desk August 20, 2026 · Updated September 15, 2026 13 min read
GooglePixelTensor G6Phone Reviews

Google's Pixel 11 lineup, the standard Pixel 11, Pixel 11 Pro, Pixel 11 Pro XL, and foldable Pixel 11 Pro Fold, has now been out for almost a month, and independent reviews have filled in the picture Google's own announcement didn't cover. Every model runs the new Tensor G6 chip and leans on Gemini AI features, but the chip is a mixed bag: genuinely better at everyday tasks and AI, but still clearly behind rival flagships in gaming. Here's what actually changed, what the reviews found, and which model is worth your money.

Announced on August 12 and released on August 20, 2026, the Pixel 11 series gives every model in the family the same Tensor G6 chipset, the same Gemini-driven software features, and Qi2 magnetic wireless charging for the first time across the whole lineup, including the base model. Every phone also got about $100 more expensive than its Pixel 10 equivalent, a bump Google and reviewers have both linked partly to the ongoing RAM supply shortage affecting the entire phone industry. In exchange, every model now starts with 256GB of storage, double the old 128GB base.

Tensor G6: Better Efficiency, Still Behind on Raw Power

Google put the same Tensor G6 chipset in every Pixel 11 model, including the standard Pixel 11, closing a gap that used to separate the base phone from the Pro models. Google's own figures claim roughly 25% faster web browsing, 15% faster app launches, and about 50% more TPU compute for on-device AI tasks compared to the Tensor G5. Independent reviewers who ran their own benchmarks found real gains too: one outlet measured a 21% jump in multi-core and 17% in single-core Geekbench 6 scores on the Pixel 11 Pro over the Pixel 10 Pro, and another recorded 2,639 single-core and 7,071 multi-core points, up from 2,282 and 6,249 on last year's phone.

The catch is graphics performance. The chip's GPU is described by multiple reviewers as a step sideways rather than forward, and in gaming benchmarks the Tensor G6 has landed roughly 30 to 50% behind Qualcomm's Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5, the chip powering most rival 2026 Android flagships. Some reviewers also noted the Tensor G6 dropped a CPU core compared to the Tensor G5 and lacks support for some newer Vulkan graphics features, which causes a handful of demanding games to automatically turn off their highest graphics settings. In practice, that means everyday use, browsing, multitasking, and Gemini-powered features all feel snappy, but anyone who games heavily on their phone will notice the difference against a Snapdragon-powered rival.

One quieter but genuinely useful change: the Tensor G6 is the first Tensor chip to drop Samsung's Exynos modem in favor of a MediaTek M90 modem. Reviewers who compared it against the Pixel 10 Pro reported the Pixel 11 Pro holding onto 5G connections longer, downloading faster, and using less battery on cellular data, addressing a long-running complaint about Pixel connectivity.

Storage and RAM also shifted this year. Every model now starts at 256GB, but Google trimmed the base RAM on that entry tier for the Pro models to 12GB, down from 16GB last generation; stepping up to the 512GB or 1TB storage option restores 16GB of RAM. The Pixel 11 Pro Fold is the exception, shipping with 16GB of RAM across all three storage tiers.

Cameras: A Bigger Main Sensor, Not a New Telephoto

The standard Pixel 11 keeps the same 13MP ultrawide and 10.8MP 5x telephoto lenses introduced on last year's Pixel 10, rather than gaining a new telephoto camera. The actual upgrade is the main camera, which moves to a larger 1/1.56-inch, 48MP sensor, up from the smaller 1/2-inch sensor on the Pixel 10, which Google says lets in about 56% more light. Combined with the carried-over telephoto, the Pixel 11 still offers a genuine triple-camera system, just built around a better main sensor rather than new hardware across the board.

The Pixel 11 Pro and Pro XL share an identical camera setup: a 50MP main camera on a 1/1.3-inch sensor, a 48MP ultrawide, and a 48MP telephoto that moves to a larger 1/1.95-inch sensor for about 30% more light than last year's Pro models. That telephoto now supports Portrait Mode at its native 5x zoom and reaches up to 120x with Google's AI-assisted Pro Res Zoom, up from 100x on the Pixel 10 Pro. Both Pro phones also get an upgraded 42MP selfie camera. The Pixel 11 Pro Fold carries the same 48MP main sensor as the standard Pixel 11, paired with a 10.5MP ultrawide and a 10.8MP 5x telephoto offering up to 30x Super Zoom, plus a 10MP selfie camera on both the cover and inner displays.

Across the lineup, Google also sped up Night Sight, which it says now captures at roughly 4.5 times the speed of previous Pixel phones, and added Magic Capture, a Gemini-powered mode that automatically identifies and saves the best moment from a burst without requiring perfectly timed shutter taps.

HiLight and the New Software Features

The Pixel 11 Pro, Pro XL, and Pro Fold introduce HiLight, a ring of RGB LEDs built into the rear flash module that glows to indicate an incoming call or an active Gemini session when the phone is face down. It's the headline design change reviewers point to when comparing the Pro models to the standard Pixel 11, but the reaction to it has been lukewarm. Multiple reviewers who tested all four phones described HiLight as a nice-to-have rather than a meaningful upgrade, since it only works in one specific orientation and offers limited practical information beyond what a notification light already conveys. The standard Pixel 11 skips HiLight entirely, and its regular flash is, if anything, slightly brighter as a result.

More substantial than HiLight is Rambler, a new on-device voice-to-text model, and Gemini Proactive Assistance, a feature designed to surface helpful suggestions before you ask for them by cross-referencing your calendar, messages, and contacts. Reviews have been split on Proactive Assistance specifically, with some calling it a genuinely useful step toward a more anticipatory assistant and others saying it hasn't yet found a killer use case worth the added background data access.

Editor's Note The most telling verdict from early reviews isn't about any single feature, it's that several reviewers who tested the whole lineup ended up preferring the cheaper Pixel 11 over the Pro models, since it now shares the same chip, similar battery life, and a genuinely improved main camera for $200 to $400 less.

Charging and Battery

Qi2 magnetic wireless charging is standard across the entire Pixel 11 lineup for the first time, including the base Pixel 11, which now supports 25W Qi2.2 wireless charging alongside 30W wired charging that Google says reaches 50% in about 30 minutes. The Pro and Pro XL charge wirelessly at the same 25W Qi2.2 speed, which Google describes as roughly 29% faster than the Pixel 10 Pro, while hitting 75% in 30 minutes over a wired connection. Battery capacity scales with size: the standard Pixel 11 carries a 4,985mAh cell, the Pixel 11 Pro roughly 4,850mAh, and the Pixel 11 Pro XL 5,115mAh. Real-world impressions from reviewers have generally been positive, with several noting they comfortably cleared a full day and, in one case, went from Saturday morning to Sunday evening on a single charge with light-to-moderate use.

Pixel 11 Pro Fold: Sharper Software, Modest Hardware Gains

The Pixel 11 Pro Fold is thinner and lighter than the outgoing Pixel 10 Pro Fold at 239 grams, and both its 8.0-inch inner display and 6.5-inch cover display are now LTPO OLED panels reaching up to 3,600 nits of peak brightness, matching the non-folding Pro models. Google also carried over IP68 dust and water resistance, keeping the Fold's durability rating in line with a standard flagship rather than trailing behind it, as most foldables have historically done.

Wireless charging is the clearest upgrade: Qi2.2 wireless speeds jump from 15W on the Pixel 10 Pro Fold to 25W here, while wired charging stays at 30W. Battery capacity actually dropped slightly year over year, to roughly 4,800mAh from about 5,015mAh, a change Google attributes to the thinner body. In review testing, that translated to shorter screen-on video playback than direct rivals like the Motorola Razr Fold and Samsung's Galaxy Z Fold 8, although real-world daily use has still generally lasted a full day for most reviewers. Some reviews also flagged the Fold's PWM dimming rate as noticeably worse than other foldables at low brightness, which may bother people who are sensitive to display flicker.

Which Pixel 11 Should You Buy?

If you want the best value in the lineup, the standard Pixel 11 is the one most reviewers keep coming back to. It shares the same Tensor G6 chip, the same 12GB of RAM at its base tier, and a genuinely improved main camera with the Pro models, while costing $200 less than the Pixel 11 Pro and skipping a feature, HiLight, that hasn't impressed many people testing it anyway.

The Pixel 11 Pro makes sense if you specifically want the longer telephoto reach, the larger ultrawide sensor, the upgraded 42MP selfie camera, or a smaller phone with a sharper LTPO display, and don't mind paying extra for those Pro-only camera upgrades. The Pixel 11 Pro XL is the pick if you want the largest screen and biggest battery without moving to a foldable, and the Pixel 11 Pro Fold is worth considering only if the multitasking and productivity benefits of a book-style foldable matter more to you than having the longest battery life or fastest charging speeds in the lineup, both of which trail dedicated non-folding flagships and some rival foldables.

Anyone gaming heavily on their phone, regardless of which Pixel 11 model, should go in with realistic expectations: the Tensor G6's CPU and AI performance are genuinely improved, but its graphics performance still lags meaningfully behind Snapdragon-powered rivals at a similar price.

Price and Availability

The Pixel 11 starts at $899 for 256GB, rising to $1,019 for 512GB. The Pixel 11 Pro starts at $1,099, the Pixel 11 Pro XL at $1,299, and the Pixel 11 Pro Fold at $1,899, each about $100 more than its Pixel 10 equivalent. All four phones became available starting August 20, 2026, through Google's own store and major carriers, with broader retail availability and carrier-specific promotions continuing to roll out in the weeks since launch.

People Also Ask

When was the Google Pixel 11 series released?
Google announced the Pixel 11, Pixel 11 Pro, Pixel 11 Pro XL, and Pixel 11 Pro Fold on August 12, 2026, with all four going on sale August 20, 2026.
What chip does the Pixel 11 series use?
Every phone in the Pixel 11 lineup uses Google's new Tensor G6 chipset, built on a 3nm process, alongside a new MediaTek M90 modem that replaces the Exynos modem used in past Tensor chips.
How much does the Google Pixel 11 series cost?
The Pixel 11 starts at $899, the Pixel 11 Pro at $1,099, the Pixel 11 Pro XL at $1,299, and the Pixel 11 Pro Fold at $1,899. Every model costs about $100 more than its Pixel 10 equivalent.
Did the standard Pixel 11 get a new telephoto camera?
No. The 10.8MP 5x telephoto lens on the standard Pixel 11 carries over unchanged from the Pixel 10. The real camera upgrade on the base model is a larger 48MP main sensor that Google says lets in about 56% more light.
Is the Tensor G6 as fast as the Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5?
Not in raw performance. Independent testing shows the Tensor G6 trailing Qualcomm's Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 by a wide margin in graphics-heavy tasks and gaming, even though everyday CPU tasks and AI features run smoothly.
What is HiLight on the Pixel 11 Pro?
HiLight is a ring of RGB LEDs in the rear flash module on the Pro, Pro XL, and Pro Fold that glows to signal an incoming call or an active Gemini session when the phone is face down. Reviewers have generally found it underwhelming in practice.
Does the base Pixel 11 support wireless charging?
Yes, for the first time the standard Pixel 11 supports 25W Qi2.2 wireless charging, alongside 30W wired charging, matching capabilities that were previously Pro-exclusive.
Is the Pixel 11 Pro Fold water resistant?
Yes, the Pixel 11 Pro Fold carries an IP68 rating, giving it dust and water resistance on par with a standard, non-folding flagship phone.
Why did Google reduce RAM on some Pixel 11 Pro models?
The 256GB storage tier of the Pixel 11 Pro and Pro XL ships with 12GB of RAM, down from 16GB on last year's entry models, a change widely linked to industry-wide RAM supply shortages. Stepping up to 512GB or 1TB restores 16GB of RAM.
Which Pixel 11 model offers the best value?
Several reviewers who tested the full lineup favored the standard Pixel 11, since it shares the Tensor G6 chip and a meaningfully improved main camera with the Pro models while costing significantly less.

Conclusion

The Pixel 11 series narrows the gap between Google's cheapest and most expensive flagships, giving every model the same chip, the same AI features, and fast wireless charging for the first time. But a month of independent testing has also surfaced real trade-offs: the Tensor G6 is a genuine step forward for everyday performance and connectivity, yet still a clear step behind rival chips in gaming, and the entry-tier RAM cut on the Pro models takes back some of what the price increase is asking for. For most buyers, the standard Pixel 11 has emerged as the strongest value in the lineup, while the Pro, Pro XL, and Pro Fold each earn their higher prices only if their specific camera, screen, or form-factor advantages actually matter to you.
