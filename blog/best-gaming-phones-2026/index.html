Best Gaming Phones of 2026: Top Picks for Every Budget

Tech Editor August 12, 2026 · Updated September 15, 2026 16 min read
REDMAGICASUS ROGOnePlusBuying Guide

Mobile games are becoming more demanding every year. Games like PUBG Mobile, Call of Duty Mobile, Genshin Impact, Honkai: Star Rail, Wuthering Waves, and newer console-style titles can push a smartphone much harder than normal apps, which is why buying a phone with a fast processor is no longer enough.

A good gaming phone needs strong graphics performance, reliable cooling, a responsive display, good battery life, fast charging, and enough storage for large games. For competitive players, features like shoulder triggers and high touch sampling rates can also make a difference.

The good news is you don't need to spend over $1,000 to get a great gaming experience in 2026. There are dedicated gaming phones built specifically for long sessions, while phones such as the OnePlus 15, iQOO 15, and POCO F8 Pro offer serious gaming performance without looking like gaming devices. In this guide, we look at the best gaming phones of 2026 for different budgets and gaming styles.

1. REDMAGIC 11S Pro — Best Gaming Phone for Dedicated Gamers

If your main reason for buying a phone is gaming, the REDMAGIC 11S Pro is built around exactly that purpose. It combines Qualcomm's Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 Leading Version with REDMAGIC's RedCore R4 gaming chip, backed by an aggressive cooling system designed to keep performance stable during long sessions: Reinforced AquaCore liquid cooling, a 13,116mm² 3D vapor chamber, Liquid Metal 3.0, and a 24,000 RPM high-speed fan.

That matters because sustained performance is different from short benchmark performance. A phone can be extremely fast for the first few minutes and then reduce performance once temperatures rise, and the REDMAGIC 11S Pro is designed specifically to fight that problem.

Display

The phone has a 6.85-inch BOE X10 AMOLED display with a resolution of 2688x1216 and a refresh rate of up to 144Hz, plus extremely high touch sampling capabilities reaching up to 3,000Hz in supported gaming scenarios, particularly useful for competitive games where quick movement and rapid touch input matter.

Battery and controls

The 7,500mAh battery is another major advantage, supporting up to 80W wired and wireless charging with reverse charging support. The phone also has 520Hz shoulder triggers, an IPX8 rating, stereo speakers, and gaming-focused software.

The downside is that this is clearly a gaming phone. Its camera system isn't the main reason to buy it, and the large cooling hardware makes it less focused on being a slim everyday flagship. For someone who spends hours gaming every day, however, those compromises can make sense.

Best for: Competitive players and people who want dedicated gaming hardware.

2. REDMAGIC 11 Pro — Best Value High-End Gaming Phone

The REDMAGIC 11 Pro is another serious option for gamers who want flagship performance without paying mainly for camera features. It uses the Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 and RedCore R4 gaming chip, and depending on the configuration can be equipped with up to 24GB of RAM and 1TB of UFS 4.1 Pro storage.

The phone's cooling system is one of its biggest advantages: a 13,116mm² 3D vapor chamber, Liquid Metal 3.0, high-conductivity materials, a 24,000 RPM fan, and a liquid-cooling system to help control temperatures during demanding workloads. In independent testing, PhoneArena rated the REDMAGIC 11 Pro's battery life as the best in the industry for its price class, alongside blazing-fast performance, while noting its gaming aesthetics and mediocre camera system as trade-offs.

Gaming display

The 6.85-inch AMOLED panel runs at up to 144Hz, with touch response reaching extremely high levels in supported modes, useful for games where every touch matters, especially competitive shooters and multiplayer games.

Huge battery

The 7,500mAh battery supports up to 80W wired and 80W wireless charging, making the REDMAGIC 11 Pro particularly attractive to gamers who don't want to constantly search for a charger.

There are compromises: the cameras are more ordinary than those on expensive mainstream flagships, and software support isn't the main reason to buy the device. Still, if gaming performance is your priority, the hardware is difficult to ignore.

Best for: Gamers who want flagship-level performance and serious cooling at a relatively competitive price.

3. ASUS ROG Phone 9 Pro — Best Gaming Features

The ASUS ROG Phone 9 Pro takes a different approach. Instead of focusing only on raw specifications, ASUS has built a complete gaming ecosystem around the phone. It uses the Snapdragon 8 Elite processor with an Adreno 830 GPU, offering up to 24GB of LPDDR5X RAM and up to 1TB of UFS 4.0 storage.

185Hz gaming display

One standout feature is the 6.78-inch AMOLED display, which can run at up to 185Hz through Game Genie, with a 720Hz touch sampling rate, adaptive refresh rates, and a peak brightness of 2,500 nits. For competitive games, a fast display and responsive touch system can make controls feel much more immediate.

AirTriggers

The ROG Phone 9 Pro also includes ASUS AirTriggers, shoulder-mounted controls that let players map actions to physical-style touch triggers, especially useful in shooters since you can keep more fingers on the screen while assigning important actions to the triggers. The phone also includes Game Genie features such as macros, bypass charging, background mode, touch controls, key mapping, and other gaming tools, plus a distinctive Mini-LED matrix on the back that can display notifications, battery percentage, or even simple games.

Battery

The phone has a 5,800mAh battery and supports 65W wired charging, with support for bypass charging that can help reduce battery stress during long gaming sessions. The ROG Phone 9 Pro is more than just a powerful phone; it's designed to feel closer to a dedicated mobile gaming device.

Best for: Players who want physical-style gaming controls and a complete gaming feature set.

4. OnePlus 15 — Best Gaming Phone for Everyday Use

Not everyone wants a phone that looks or feels like a gaming device. If you want excellent gaming performance but also care about cameras, software, battery life, and everyday usability, the OnePlus 15 is an interesting choice. It uses the Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 with 12GB or 16GB of LPDDR5X memory depending on configuration, and storage options including 256GB and 512GB of UFS 4.1.

165Hz gaming display

The 6.78-inch LTPO display supports up to 165Hz in gaming mode. OnePlus specifically highlights 120fps gaming support in compatible games, with its lab testing reporting that Mobile Legends: Bang Bang can maintain 120fps for at least 60 minutes under its test conditions.

7,300mAh battery

Battery life is another major strength. The OnePlus 15 has a 7,300mAh silicon-carbon battery with fast wired and wireless charging (up to 120W wired and 50W wireless globally, with US units capped lower), a large battery that's useful for gamers because demanding 3D games can drain a phone much faster than social media or video playback. OnePlus also gives the phone a gaming-focused gyroscope and cooling system, resulting in a phone that can handle gaming while still feeling like a normal premium smartphone.

Best for: People who want one phone for gaming, photography, social media, and everyday use.

5. iQOO 15 — Best for Performance and Battery

The iQOO 15 is another phone worth considering if gaming performance is the main priority. It uses the Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 on a 3nm process, with up to 16GB of RAM and 1TB of storage, using LPDDR5X memory and UFS 4.1 storage. The display is a 6.85-inch AMOLED panel with a 144Hz refresh rate and a 3168x1440 resolution, giving the phone both high refresh-rate gaming and a sharp display for normal use.

Large 7,000mAh battery

The iQOO 15 also has a 7,000mAh battery with 100W charging, making it particularly interesting for gamers who care about battery endurance as much as frame rates. It also carries IP68 and IP69 ratings, providing stronger dust and water protection than many dedicated gaming phones. The main issue is availability, since iQOO's global reach varies by market, so buyers should check whether the specific model and network bands suit their country.

Best for: Gamers who want high-end performance, a sharp 144Hz screen, and a large battery.

6. POCO F8 Pro — Best Affordable Flagship Gaming Phone

You don't always need the latest Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 to get excellent gaming performance. The POCO F8 Pro uses the original Snapdragon 8 Elite, still a powerful flagship processor built on a 3nm process with two prime cores reaching up to 4.32GHz and six performance cores reaching up to 3.53GHz, paired with an Adreno 830 GPU. The phone comes with 12GB of LPDDR5X memory and up to 512GB of UFS 4.1 storage.

Gaming display

The 6.59-inch AMOLED display supports up to 120Hz refresh rate. More importantly for gaming, its Game Turbo mode can increase instant touch sampling to 2,560Hz under supported conditions.

The POCO F8 Pro packs a 6,210mAh battery with up to 100W HyperCharge wired charging, reaching a full charge in around 40 minutes, though it notably skips wireless charging entirely. The phone carries an IP68 rating and Bose-tuned stereo speakers. Independent reviews have praised its flagship-level performance, bright display, and decent battery runtime, while flagging its ultrawide and low-light camera performance and lack of wireless charging as the main compromises.

The POCO F8 Pro's biggest advantage is its combination of flagship-class processing power and a lower price than many traditional flagship phones.

Best for: Gamers who want strong flagship performance without paying flagship-level prices.

7. Nothing Phone (4a) Pro — Best Mid-Range Gaming Phone

For players with a smaller budget, the Nothing Phone (4a) Pro is an interesting option. It doesn't compete with the Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 phones in raw performance, but it focuses on delivering a smooth gaming experience at a lower price. The Phone (4a) Pro uses the Snapdragon 7 Gen 4 with LPDDR5X memory and UFS 3.1 storage, alongside a 144Hz adaptive display.

Designed for gaming

Nothing claims the Phone (4a) Pro can deliver 120fps gaming for more than 60 minutes in supported scenarios, backed by a 2,500Hz instant touch sampling rate and a vapor-cooling system. The aluminium unibody also contributes to heat management, with Nothing saying its cooling area is significantly larger than the previous generation.

This isn't the phone to choose if you want maximum graphics performance in every demanding game, but it can make more sense for someone who wants a stylish everyday phone that can also handle mobile gaming.

Best for: Mid-range buyers who want gaming capability without buying a dedicated gaming phone.

8. Samsung Galaxy S26 Ultra — Best Gaming Phone for a Complete Flagship Experience

Dedicated gaming phones aren't the only option. The Samsung Galaxy S26 Ultra is a powerful mainstream flagship that can also handle demanding games while giving you a much stronger camera system, premium build, long-term software ecosystem, and broader everyday feature set. It's especially interesting for people who play games but also use their phone heavily for photography, work, and media.

The main advantage here is balance: instead of buying a gaming phone with average cameras, you get a premium all-purpose smartphone that can also deliver high-end gaming performance. Recent 2026 testing and phone roundups continue to place the Galaxy S26 Ultra among the strongest Android flagships, with its performance and cooling helping it handle demanding workloads.

The downside is that Samsung doesn't provide the same dedicated gaming hardware as REDMAGIC or ROG; you don't get built-in shoulder triggers or an internal high-speed fan. Still, if gaming is only one part of what you want from your phone, the Galaxy S26 Ultra offers a different type of gaming experience.

Best for: Gamers who also want a premium camera and everyday flagship.

9. iPhone 18 Pro Max — Best Gaming iPhone

For iOS gamers, the iPhone 18 Pro Max deserves attention. Apple's A20 Pro chip gives the phone the processing and graphics power needed for demanding games, while hardware-supported ray tracing and Apple's graphics technologies enable more advanced visual effects. The iPhone 18 Pro Max also has a large 6.9-inch OLED display with ProMotion up to 120Hz.

Its advantage isn't gaming-specific hardware; instead, it benefits from Apple's powerful chip design, optimized software ecosystem, and strong game support. The biggest difference compared with Android gaming phones is the control setup: there are no built-in AirTriggers like the ROG Phone 9 Pro and no internal high-speed cooling fan like REDMAGIC. However, for someone who already prefers iOS, the iPhone 18 Pro Max provides a powerful gaming platform without requiring a dedicated gaming phone. For the full breakdown of what's new on this generation, see our iPhone 18 Pro Max specs and release coverage.

Best for: iOS users who want maximum iPhone performance for gaming.

What Makes a Good Gaming Phone in 2026?

Before buying a gaming phone, look beyond the processor name. Several other components determine how good the gaming experience will actually be.

1. Processor and GPU

The processor is obviously important. Flagship chips such as the Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 provide the highest level of Android performance, while older flagship chips such as the Snapdragon 8 Elite can still deliver excellent results. However, the GPU matters just as much as the CPU for games, and a phone with a powerful CPU but poor thermal management can still struggle during long gaming sessions.

2. Cooling

Cooling is one of the biggest differences between a normal flagship and a dedicated gaming phone. Gaming produces a lot of heat, and once temperatures become too high, a phone may reduce performance to protect the hardware, a process called thermal throttling. REDMAGIC addresses this with a combination of liquid cooling, vapor chambers, liquid metal, and an active fan, while ROG also focuses heavily on thermal management and gaming accessories. For someone who plays for several hours at a time, sustained performance can matter more than a short benchmark score.

3. Display Refresh Rate

A 120Hz display is already very smooth. Gaming phones can go further, with devices such as the ROG Phone 9 Pro reaching 185Hz and the OnePlus 15 reaching 165Hz in gaming mode. But a high refresh rate doesn't automatically mean a game will run at the same frame rate; the game itself must support higher FPS, and the processor must be able to render those frames.

4. Touch Sampling

Touch response is especially important in competitive games. A high touch sampling rate lets the phone detect touch input more frequently, which can make aiming, swiping, and tapping feel more responsive. However, manufacturers use different testing methods and modes for these numbers, so they shouldn't be compared as if every advertised figure represents the same measurement.

5. Battery

Gaming can drain a battery quickly. A larger battery gives you more room for long sessions, while fast charging means you can get back into the game quickly. The REDMAGIC 11 Pro and 11S Pro both use 7,500mAh batteries, the OnePlus 15 has a 7,300mAh battery, and the iQOO 15 has a 7,000mAh battery.

6. Storage

Modern mobile games can take up a lot of space. If you install several large games, 128GB can become uncomfortable very quickly. For serious gamers, 256GB should be considered a practical starting point, while 512GB gives you considerably more room.

Dedicated Gaming Phone vs Normal Flagship

Should you buy a dedicated gaming phone or a normal flagship? It depends on what you do with your phone.

Choose a dedicated gaming phone if:

  • You play for several hours every day
  • Sustained performance is extremely important
  • You want shoulder triggers
  • You want advanced cooling
  • You care about gaming-specific software
  • You want a headphone jack
  • You want a very high refresh-rate display

REDMAGIC and ROG are examples of this approach.

Choose a normal flagship if:

  • You play games but also care about cameras
  • You want a thinner and more conventional design
  • You need strong water resistance
  • You care about long software support
  • You use your phone for work and photography
  • You don't need physical gaming controls

The OnePlus 15, Galaxy S26 Ultra, and iPhone 18 Pro Max fit this category.

How Much RAM Do You Need for Gaming?

You don't necessarily need 24GB of RAM to play mobile games. For most users, 8GB or 12GB can be enough, though 12GB or 16GB is useful if you regularly switch between demanding games and other applications. Phones such as the REDMAGIC 11 Pro and ROG Phone 9 Pro offer configurations with up to 24GB RAM, but more RAM doesn't automatically make a game run at a higher frame rate; the processor, GPU, cooling system, and game optimization still matter.

Is a Gaming Phone Worth Buying in 2026?

A dedicated gaming phone can be worth considering if gaming is one of the main things you do with your smartphone. The biggest advantage isn't always the highest benchmark score; it's the combination of sustained performance, cooling, controls, battery capacity, and gaming software. For casual gamers, however, a normal flagship or affordable performance phone may make more sense. You can get excellent gaming performance from phones such as the OnePlus 15, iQOO 15, and POCO F8 Pro while also getting better cameras and a more conventional smartphone design. The important thing is to match the phone with your actual gaming habits.

People Also Ask

What is the best gaming phone in 2026?
The answer depends on what you want from the phone. Dedicated gaming phones such as the REDMAGIC 11S Pro, REDMAGIC 11 Pro and ASUS ROG Phone 9 Pro focus heavily on cooling, controls and sustained performance, while phones such as the OnePlus 15 and Galaxy S26 Ultra combine gaming with broader everyday features.
Which phone is best for PUBG Mobile in 2026?
High-end phones with powerful GPUs, high refresh-rate displays, responsive touch systems and good cooling are suitable for PUBG Mobile. Dedicated gaming phones such as REDMAGIC and ROG also add gaming-specific controls and software.
Which phone is best for Call of Duty Mobile?
A phone with a powerful GPU, high refresh-rate display, fast touch response and stable thermal performance is a good fit for Call of Duty Mobile. The REDMAGIC and ROG lines are designed specifically around these requirements.
Is 120Hz enough for gaming?
Yes. A 120Hz display is already very smooth and is enough for most mobile gamers. Higher refresh rates such as 144Hz, 165Hz and 185Hz can provide additional smoothness when the game and hardware support those frame rates.
Is 12GB RAM enough for gaming?
Yes. 12GB of RAM is more than enough for most mobile gaming. Higher capacities such as 16GB or 24GB can help with heavy multitasking, but they do not automatically produce higher gaming FPS.
Is a gaming phone better than a flagship phone?
Not necessarily. A dedicated gaming phone usually offers more gaming-specific hardware, while a mainstream flagship often provides a stronger combination of cameras, software, design, water resistance and everyday features.

Conclusion

The best gaming phone isn't automatically the phone with the biggest benchmark number. In 2026, gamers have more choices than ever. Dedicated phones such as the REDMAGIC 11S Pro, REDMAGIC 11 Pro, and ASUS ROG Phone 9 Pro focus on cooling, high-refresh-rate displays, gaming controls, and long sessions. Meanwhile, the OnePlus 15, iQOO 15, and POCO F8 Pro show that a phone doesn't need an aggressive gaming design to deliver powerful performance.

For buyers who want gaming alongside photography and everyday features, mainstream flagships such as the Galaxy S26 Ultra and iPhone 18 Pro Max remain relevant choices. Before buying, consider the games you actually play, how long you play each day, your budget, battery requirements, and whether you need gaming-specific features such as shoulder triggers. That will help you find the best gaming phone for your own needs, rather than simply choosing the phone with the biggest specification sheet.
