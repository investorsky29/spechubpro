Best Foldable Phones of 2026: Buying Guide

Tech Editor August 25, 2026 · Updated September 15, 2026 17 min read
SamsungHONORGoogleBuying Guide

Foldable phones have changed a lot in the last few years. What started as an expensive experiment has become a real smartphone category, with several companies now offering foldable phones for different types of users. In 2026, you can choose between large book-style foldables designed for productivity and smaller flip phones that fold into a much more pocket-friendly size.

The latest models are also thinner, lighter, and more durable than many of the early foldables. Some now have batteries that can compete with traditional flagship phones, while newer processors provide enough performance for gaming, multitasking, and demanding apps. That doesn't mean every foldable phone is worth buying, though. Foldables still cost more than many conventional smartphones, and the folding display and hinge introduce extra considerations that you don't have with a normal phone.

So, what are the best foldable phones in 2026? This guide looks at the most interesting foldable phones available now, including large book-style models, compact flip phones, and devices that focus on battery life, cameras, productivity, and value.

1. Samsung Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra

Best foldable phone for productivity and high-end performance

The Samsung Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra is one of the biggest changes to Samsung's foldable lineup in 2026. Instead of simply making another version of the regular Fold, Samsung introduced the first Ultra model in the Z Fold series at its Galaxy Unpacked event on July 22, 2026, releasing it on August 7. It has an 8-inch internal display, a 6.5-inch cover screen, and a Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 for Galaxy processor.

The phone is also surprisingly thin for a large foldable. When unfolded, it measures just 4.1mm thick and weighs 215 grams, matching the profile of the Galaxy Z Fold7 despite the added Ultra branding. Samsung uses two titanium layers beneath the flexible display to help strengthen the structure, along with a smoother Armor FlexHinge and an IP48 rating.

Key specifications

  • 8.0-inch Dynamic AMOLED 2X inner display, 6.5-inch cover display, both up to 3,000 nits peak brightness
  • 1-120Hz adaptive refresh rate on both screens
  • Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 for Galaxy
  • 12GB or 16GB RAM
  • 256GB, 512GB, or 1TB storage
  • 200MP main camera, 50MP ultra-wide camera, 10MP 3x telephoto camera, 8K video
  • 5,000mAh battery
  • 215g, 4.1mm unfolded
  • Android 17 / One UI
  • IP48 protection

The camera setup is also much closer to Samsung's conventional Ultra phones than previous Fold models, including a 200MP main camera, an upgraded 50MP ultra-wide, and a 10MP 3x telephoto camera working with Samsung's ProVisual Engine. The 5,000mAh battery is another major upgrade, with Samsung rating the phone for up to 27 hours of video playback.

Who should buy it?

The Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra makes the most sense if you want a foldable that can act almost like a small tablet, useful for multitasking, reading documents, watching videos, editing photos, gaming, working with two apps at once via two-way and three-way Split View, and browsing desktop-style websites. Samsung also supports Multi Window, Samsung DeX, and Galaxy AI features on its foldables. One thing to remember is that the Z Fold8 Ultra does not support the S Pen, despite its productivity-focused design.

2. Samsung Galaxy Z Fold8

Best balanced book-style foldable

If the Fold8 Ultra is more than you need, the regular Galaxy Z Fold8 provides a smaller and lighter alternative. Samsung redesigned the Fold8 around a 5.5-inch cover screen and a 7.6-inch internal display. The phone weighs only 201 grams, making it the lightest Galaxy Z Fold model in Samsung's lineup. The new 10:16 cover screen is designed to feel more natural for everyday phone use, while the 4:3 inner display gives you more space for videos, reading, and multitasking.

Key specifications

  • 5.5-inch cover display, 7.6-inch Dynamic AMOLED 2X inner display
  • 1-120Hz refresh rate
  • Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 for Galaxy
  • 12GB RAM
  • 256GB, 512GB, or 1TB storage
  • 50MP main camera, 50MP ultra-wide camera, 10MP cover camera
  • 4,800mAh battery
  • 201g
  • Android 17 / One UI

Samsung rates the battery for up to 26 hours of video playback. The Fold8 also includes Samsung's newer Armor FlexHinge and a dual-layer titanium structure under the inner display, with Samsung saying the hinge has been tested for up to 200,000 folds.

Why consider the Fold8?

It offers a good middle ground: you get the large folding screen and flagship performance without moving all the way to the larger Fold8 Ultra. For people who want one device for both phone and tablet-style tasks, it's one of the most practical foldable designs available in 2026.

3. HONOR Magic V6

Best foldable phone for battery life

The HONOR Magic V6 is one of the most interesting alternatives to Samsung. Its biggest advantage is the battery: HONOR puts a 6,660mAh silicon-carbon battery inside the Magic V6, unusually large for a foldable, supporting 80W wired charging and 66W wireless charging. That combination makes the Magic V6 particularly interesting for people who spend long hours using the large inner display.

Key specifications

  • 6.52-inch outer display, 7.95-inch inner display
  • 1-120Hz adaptive refresh rate
  • Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5
  • Up to 16GB RAM, up to 1TB storage
  • 50MP main camera, 50MP ultra-wide, 64MP telephoto
  • 6,660mAh battery, 80W wired / 66W wireless charging
  • 219g
  • Android 16 / MagicOS 10
  • IP68/IP69

The Magic V6 is also very thin: HONOR lists it at 8.75mm when folded and around 219g, with the inner display opening to around 4mm. The phone also has IP68 and IP69 protection and a hinge tested for up to 500,000 folds.

Why is the Magic V6 interesting?

The combination of a huge battery, fast charging, and slim body addresses one of the biggest weaknesses of foldable phones: you get a large display without carrying an extremely heavy device. HONOR also gives the phone a 64MP telephoto camera with stabilization and supports stylus input on its displays. For buyers who care about battery life and portability, the Magic V6 deserves serious attention.

4. Google Pixel 11 Pro Fold

Best foldable phone for Google AI and photography

Google's Pixel 11 Pro Fold takes a different approach. Rather than focusing mainly on hardware specifications, Google combines the folding design with its Pixel camera processing, Gemini features, and Tensor G6 platform. The phone has a 6.5-inch outer display and an 8-inch inner folding display, both supporting 1-120Hz refresh rates.

Key specifications

  • 6.5-inch outer OLED display, 8-inch inner OLED display
  • 1-120Hz refresh rate
  • Google Tensor G6
  • 16GB RAM across all storage tiers
  • 256GB, 512GB, or 1TB storage
  • 48MP main camera, 10.5MP ultra-wide, 10.8MP 5x telephoto, up to 30x Super Zoom
  • Roughly 4,800mAh battery
  • 30W wired charging, 25W Qi2.2 wireless charging
  • Android, with seven years of OS and security updates

Google rates the Pixel 11 Pro Fold for more than 24 hours of battery life, reaching around 50% in about 30 minutes using a compatible 30W USB-C PPS charger or faster, a notable jump from the 15W wireless charging on last year's model. The camera setup includes a 48MP wide camera, 10.5MP ultra-wide, and 10.8MP 5x telephoto, with Google's Super Zoom system reaching up to 30x. For the full lineup context, see our Pixel 11 series review.

Who is it for?

The Pixel 11 Pro Fold is especially interesting for people who want Google's camera processing, Gemini and Pixel AI features, a large folding screen, clean Android software, strong photography, and Google's security features. It's also a good option if you already use other Google services heavily.

5. Motorola Razr Ultra (2026)

Best premium flip phone

Not everyone wants a foldable that opens into a mini tablet. For some people, the whole point of a foldable phone is simply being able to make a normal smartphone much smaller, and that's where flip phones come in. The Motorola Razr Ultra (2026) is one of the premium clamshell foldables available in 2026; WIRED includes it among its recommended folding phones, alongside the Galaxy Z Fold8, Pixel 11 Pro Fold, and other current models.

The basic idea is simple: you get a normal-sized smartphone display when the phone is open, but it folds vertically so it takes up less space in your pocket, making the Razr Ultra particularly useful for people who care about portability.

Why buy a premium flip phone?

A flip phone can give you a large screen when open, a compact body when closed, quick access to notifications, Flex-mode photography, hands-free video calls, and a more distinctive design. The downside is that flip phones generally don't provide the same large tablet-style workspace as book-style foldables. If productivity is your priority, a Fold-style device makes more sense; if pocketability is your priority, a Razr-style phone is easier to live with.

6. Samsung Galaxy Z Flip8

Best Samsung flip phone

The Galaxy Z Flip8 is Samsung's 2026 compact foldable. It has a 6.9-inch main display and a 4.1-inch FlexWindow cover display, weighing 180 grams and measuring just 6.1mm thick when open.

Key specifications

  • 6.9-inch main display, 4.1-inch cover display
  • Up to 120Hz refresh rate
  • Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 for Galaxy
  • 8GB RAM
  • 256GB / 512GB storage
  • 50MP main camera, 12MP ultra-wide, 10MP selfie camera
  • 4,300mAh battery
  • Android 17 / One UI
  • IP48

Samsung rates the Flip8 for up to 31 hours of video playback. Samsung has also improved the hinge and inner display design, with the phone rated for up to 200,000 folds.

Why choose the Flip8?

The Galaxy Z Flip8 isn't trying to replace a tablet; instead, it makes the normal smartphone smaller. You can close it and put it into a smaller pocket, then open it whenever you need the full 6.9-inch display, making it particularly attractive to users who want a foldable mainly for convenience and style rather than productivity.

7. Motorola Razr (2026)

Best more affordable flip foldable

The Motorola Razr (2026) is aimed at people who like the flip-phone concept but don't necessarily want to spend premium flagship money. WIRED lists the 2026 Razr as a budget folding option, alongside the premium Razr Ultra. The important thing about this type of phone is that you still get the basic benefits of a foldable: a compact folded size, a large display when opened, cover-screen functionality, Flex-style camera modes, and the foldable design itself. The compromises typically come in areas such as processor performance, cameras, materials, and charging compared with the company's Ultra model, a reasonable trade-off if your main reason for buying a foldable is the compact design.

8. HONOR Magic V6 vs Samsung Galaxy Z Fold8

If you're mainly interested in book-style foldables, these two phones are worth comparing. The Samsung Galaxy Z Fold8 is lighter at 201g and has a 7.6-inch inner display, while the HONOR Magic V6 is around 219g with a slightly larger 7.95-inch inner screen.

HONOR has the larger battery by a significant margin: 6,660mAh on the Magic V6 versus 4,800mAh on the Galaxy Z Fold8. HONOR also supports 80W wired and 66W wireless charging, while Samsung focuses more heavily on its software, AI, and Galaxy ecosystem. This shows why foldable buying decisions are more complicated than simply looking at screen size: one phone may have the better battery, another may have better software integration, another may have better cameras, and another may be easier to buy and service in your country.

9. What About the iPhone Fold?

Apple has finally entered the foldable market in 2026. The company officially unveiled its first foldable iPhone, the iPhone Duo, alongside the iPhone 18 Pro and iPhone 18 Pro Max on September 9, 2026. It features a 7.6-inch inner display, the largest iPhone display ever, and a 5.4-inch outer display that delivers about 90% of the screen area of the iPhone 18 Pro when closed, built around Apple's A20 Pro chip, a titanium frame with Ceramic Shield, and IP68 water and dust resistance.

Pre-orders open October 16, with the iPhone Duo becoming available on October 23, priced from $1,999 for 256GB up to $3,199 for 2TB. Apple has confirmed some notable trade-offs versus its numbered iPhones: the Duo skips Face ID in favor of a Touch ID side button and a hidden under-display FaceTime camera, and it doesn't get a telephoto lens. Because it's not yet in normal retail availability at the time of this guide, it's difficult to place it alongside phones that have already been tested and are available to buy today.

That doesn't mean it should be ignored. Apple's entry could become important for people who already own an iPhone, Mac, Apple Watch, or other Apple products, especially with Apple already lining up developer support from apps like Netflix, Zoom, and Slack. However, if you need a foldable phone right now, the current Android foldables provide more established options. See our iPhone 18 Pro Max coverage for more on the rest of Apple's September lineup.

Editor's Note At $1,999 to start, the iPhone Duo is priced well above every Android foldable in this guide. Whether that premium is worth it will likely come down to how well Apple's app ecosystem adapts to the dual-screen format, something that's hard to judge before the phone actually ships.

How to Choose the Best Foldable Phone

Buying a foldable phone requires a little more thought than buying a normal smartphone. Here are the most important things to check.

1. Decide Between Book-Style and Flip

This is the first decision. Book-style foldables, including the Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra, Galaxy Z Fold8, Pixel 11 Pro Fold, HONOR Magic V6, and iPhone Duo, open like a book, and their main advantage is the large internal display, better for multitasking, reading, gaming, watching movies, productivity, editing, large spreadsheets, and web browsing. Flip foldables, including the Galaxy Z Flip8, Motorola Razr Ultra, and Motorola Razr, fold vertically instead, and their biggest advantage is portability, better if you want a normal smartphone that becomes smaller when you're finished using it.

2. Check the Battery

Foldable phones have two displays and a hinge, so battery efficiency matters. Look beyond the mAh number and check battery capacity, processor efficiency, charging speed, wireless charging, real-world battery tests, and your expected screen-on time. The HONOR Magic V6 is particularly interesting here because of its 6,660mAh silicon-carbon battery.

3. Look at Hinge and Durability

The hinge is one of the most important parts of any foldable. Check whether the phone has water resistance, dust protection, strong hinge construction, a reinforced flexible display, a good warranty, and available replacement parts. HONOR rates the Magic V6 for up to 500,000 folds, while Samsung says its newer Z Fold8 family has been tested for up to 200,000 folds. These laboratory fold tests shouldn't be treated as a guarantee of real-world lifespan, but they provide useful information about how manufacturers test the hinge.

4. Check the Inner Display

A large internal screen is one of the main reasons to buy a foldable, so think about how you'll use it. If you mostly watch videos, an 8-inch display can be great; if you read books or documents, aspect ratio matters; if you multitask, software support becomes just as important as the physical screen. Samsung's Fold8, for example, uses a 4:3 internal screen designed around larger-screen content and multitasking.

5. Check the Cover Screen

Don't ignore the outside screen; you'll use it every time the phone is folded. A narrow cover screen can make typing and browsing uncomfortable, while a wider screen can make the phone feel much more like a normal smartphone. This is one reason Samsung changed the Fold8's cover display proportions.

6. Consider the Cameras

Foldables have improved considerably in photography, but camera hardware can still vary widely. If cameras are important, look for a large main sensor, optical image stabilization, an ultra-wide camera, a telephoto camera, a good selfie camera, high-quality video, and useful camera software. The Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra stands out with its 200MP main camera, while the Pixel 11 Pro Fold focuses heavily on Google's computational photography.

7. Think About Software Support

A foldable is expensive, so software support matters. Before buying, check how long the manufacturer promises Android updates, security updates, feature updates, and AI features, and also check how well apps support large foldable displays. A powerful processor doesn't help much if the apps you use don't take advantage of the bigger screen.

Are Foldable Phones Worth Buying in 2026?

For the right person, yes. The technology has matured considerably. Today's foldables can be thinner, lighter, and more durable than earlier generations; the Galaxy Z Fold8 is 201g, while the HONOR Magic V6 is around 219g, showing how close some large foldables are getting to the weight of conventional flagship phones.

However, there are still reasons to choose a normal smartphone. A conventional flagship is usually cheaper, simpler, more resistant to accidental damage, easier to repair, less bulky, and available with more traditional camera designs. A foldable makes the most sense when you'll actually use its folding design; if you only want a premium phone and never plan to use the large inner screen, a normal flagship may be more practical. But if you want one device that can function as both a smartphone and a small tablet, a foldable becomes much more interesting.

People Also Ask

What is the best foldable phone in 2026?
The answer depends on the type of foldable you want. Large book-style models such as the Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra, Galaxy Z Fold8, Pixel 11 Pro Fold and HONOR Magic V6 focus on productivity and large-screen use, while the Galaxy Z Flip8 and Motorola Razr models focus on portability.
Are foldable phones reliable in 2026?
Foldable phones have become more durable, but the hinge and flexible display still require more care than a traditional smartphone screen. Modern models now include stronger hinges, improved materials and water or dust resistance on some devices.
What is the best foldable phone for battery life?
The HONOR Magic V6 is one of the most battery-focused foldables in 2026, with a 6,660mAh silicon-carbon battery and 80W wired charging.
Which foldable phone has the biggest screen?
The Samsung Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra and Google Pixel 11 Pro Fold both have 8-inch internal displays, among the largest in a mainstream foldable, while Apple's iPhone Duo has a slightly smaller but still very large 7.6-inch inner display.
Is a flip phone better than a Fold?
Neither design is automatically better. Flip phones are more compact when folded, while book-style foldables provide a much larger screen for multitasking, gaming and productivity.
Is the Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra worth buying?
It can make sense if you specifically want a large foldable display, flagship performance and a high-end camera system. It is less compelling if you do not need the large inner screen or if a normal flagship would meet your needs.
Should I buy a foldable phone instead of a normal flagship?
A foldable is worth considering if you will use the larger internal screen or compact folding design regularly. If you mainly want cameras, battery life and durability at the lowest possible cost, a traditional flagship can still be a better fit.

Conclusion

The best foldable phones in 2026 are no longer defined only by the fact that their screens can bend. The newest models are competing on battery life, cameras, performance, durability, software, and everyday usability. Samsung has expanded its lineup with the Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra, Galaxy Z Fold8, and Galaxy Z Flip8. Google offers the Pixel 11 Pro Fold, HONOR's Magic V6 focuses heavily on battery capacity, thinness, and durability, and Apple has now entered the category with the iPhone Duo. Motorola continues to target the flip-phone market with its Razr family.

The most important thing is to choose the foldable design that actually fits your lifestyle. If you want a mini tablet in your pocket, look at a book-style foldable. If you simply want your phone to become smaller, look at a flip phone. If battery life is your biggest concern, pay close attention to silicon-carbon models such as the HONOR Magic V6. And if you want a premium foldable with a large display and powerful hardware, Samsung's latest Z Fold models provide some of the most complete options in the category.

Foldables are still more expensive than regular smartphones, but in 2026 they're much easier to justify than they were a few years ago. The technology has moved beyond being just a novelty. For users who genuinely benefit from the extra screen or compact folded design, a good foldable can be a useful everyday device rather than simply an expensive experiment.
