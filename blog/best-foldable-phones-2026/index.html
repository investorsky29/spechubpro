Best Foldable Phones in 2026: Worth Buying?

Tech Editor July 17, 2026 12 min read
SamsungMotorolaHonorBuying Guide

Foldable phones have finally shaken off their early reputation for being thick, fragile, and gimmicky. This guide compares the best foldable phones of 2026 on display quality, cameras, durability, and battery life, and answers the question buyers keep asking: are they actually worth the extra money now?

Foldable phones spent their first few years feeling like an expensive experiment. Early models were noticeably thicker and heavier than standard flagships, their cameras lagged behind, and a visible crease down the middle of the screen was hard to ignore. That has changed considerably. This year's foldables are thinner and lighter than several standard flagships, their cameras have closed much of the gap with numbered flagship phones, and hinge designs have made the crease far less noticeable than it used to be.

The remaining question for most buyers isn't whether foldables have gotten better, it's whether the higher price is still worth it. This guide breaks down the best option in each foldable category so you can decide for yourself.

Best Overall Foldable: Samsung Galaxy Z Fold 7

The Galaxy Z Fold 7 is the clearest sign yet that book-style foldables have matured into genuinely excellent phones rather than tech demos. Samsung made it around 26 percent thinner than the previous generation and lighter than even the Galaxy S25 Ultra, while still opening up to a spacious 8-inch internal display that comfortably runs up to three apps side by side for real multitasking.

Its 200MP main camera, paired with Samsung's ProVisual Engine, closes much of the gap that used to exist between foldable cameras and standard flagship cameras, and its Snapdragon 8 Elite for Galaxy chipset delivers flagship-level everyday performance. Starting at $1,999 for 256GB, it remains expensive, and its 4,400mAh battery capacity hasn't grown alongside the rest of the phone, but as an all-around book-style foldable, it is the one to beat this year.

Best Compact Foldable: Samsung Galaxy Z Flip 7

The Galaxy Z Flip 7 remains the most polished clamshell foldable on the market. Its 6.9-inch main display folds down into a genuinely pocketable size, while a larger 4.1-inch cover screen lets you check notifications, reply to messages, and even run select apps without fully opening the phone. At $1,099, it costs meaningfully less than the Z Fold 7 while offering an entirely different, more compact form factor.

Its 4,300mAh battery and camera system are both solid but trail behind a numbered Galaxy S flagship, a reasonable trade-off given its compact design. For buyers who want a foldable specifically for its smaller everyday footprint rather than a bigger screen, it remains the benchmark.

Best Foldable Battery Life: Motorola Razr Ultra (2026)

The Motorola Razr Ultra (2026) addresses foldables' biggest historical weakness directly. Its silicon-carbon battery delivered the longest battery life of any foldable tested this year, according to independent battery benchmarking, while still packing in a full flagship Snapdragon 8 Elite chipset and a genuinely useful 165Hz cover display for quick tasks without opening the phone.

For buyers who have avoided foldables specifically over battery anxiety, this is the model that most directly answers that concern, without asking you to compromise on everyday performance to get there.

Best Large-Display Alternative: Honor Magic V6

The Honor Magic V6 takes the book-style foldable format further than Samsung currently does, packing a large 7.95-inch internal display alongside a substantial 7,150mAh battery, bigger than most standard smartphone batteries let alone a typical foldable's. For buyers who want the largest possible unfolded screen paired with genuinely strong battery life, it is one of the more compelling alternatives to the Galaxy Z Fold 7 this year.

Its main trade-off is availability, since Honor's foldable lineup carries more limited official carrier support in some Western markets compared to Samsung. For buyers who can access it, it remains one of the most interesting foldables released in 2026.

Editor's Note Foldable durability has improved significantly, but the hinge remains the part most likely to wear over years of daily folding. If you plan to keep a foldable phone for several years, ask about official hinge repair costs before you buy.

Are Foldable Phones Actually Worth Buying in 2026?

For most buyers, the honest answer is: it depends on what you want from the extra money. Foldables in 2026 no longer ask you to sacrifice camera quality or everyday performance the way early models did, and hinge durability has improved enough that daily folding is no longer a serious long-term concern for most people. What you are still paying a premium for is the form factor itself, a larger unfolded screen or a genuinely pocketable compact design, not a meaningfully better version of a standard flagship.

If a bigger screen for multitasking, reading, or watching video genuinely appeals to you, or if a compact everyday phone that still opens into something larger sounds useful, a 2026 foldable is a much easier recommendation than it would have been just two or three years ago. If you simply want the best possible camera or battery life for the money, a standard flagship like the Galaxy S26 Ultra remains the more practical choice.

Foldable Display Technology Explained Simply

Do Foldables Still Have a Visible Crease?

Most foldable phones in 2026 still show a faint crease under certain lighting angles, though newer hinge designs from Samsung, Honor, and Motorola have made it far less noticeable during normal use than on early foldable generations. In everyday use, most owners report the crease becomes something they simply stop noticing after the first few days.

What Makes Foldable Displays More Durable Now?

Modern foldables use a combination of ultra-thin flexible glass and improved internal hinge mechanisms that distribute stress more evenly across the fold point. Samsung's move to Corning Gorilla Glass Ceramic 2 on the Galaxy Z Fold 7's cover display, and similar protective glass technology from other brands, has meaningfully improved scratch resistance compared to just a couple of generations ago.

Common Mistakes When Buying a Foldable Phone

  1. Buying based on unfolded screen size alone. A larger internal display often means a smaller, heavier cover screen experience, so consider how you'll actually use the phone closed as much as open.
  2. Ignoring hinge repair costs. The hinge is the part most likely to need repair over several years of daily use, so check official repair pricing before committing to a foldable as a long-term phone.
  3. Assuming all foldables have caught up to flagship cameras. Progress has been real, but most foldables, including the Galaxy Z Fold 7, still trail a numbered flagship's absolute best camera zoom range.
  4. Overlooking battery capacity relative to screen size. A foldable's larger display draws more power, so battery capacity that looks solid on paper can drain faster than the same number would on a standard phone.

People Also Ask

Are foldable phones worth buying in 2026?
Yes, for buyers who value a larger screen or a more compact everyday form factor and are comfortable with a higher price than a standard flagship. Foldables in 2026 are thinner, lighter, and more durable than early generations, though they still generally cost more than an equivalent non-folding flagship.
Which is the best foldable phone in 2026?
The Samsung Galaxy Z Fold 7 is the best overall book-style foldable in 2026, offering a large 8-inch internal display, a 200MP camera, and a notably thinner and lighter design than previous generations. The Galaxy Z Flip 7 remains the top pick for a compact clamshell foldable.
Do foldable phones still have a crease in the display?
Most foldable phones in 2026 still show a faint crease under certain lighting angles, though newer hinge designs from Samsung, Honor, and Motorola have made it far less noticeable than early foldable generations.
What is the best foldable phone for battery life in 2026?
The Motorola Razr Ultra (2026) offers the longest battery life of any foldable tested this year, thanks to a silicon-carbon battery paired with a full flagship Snapdragon 8 Elite chipset.
Is the Galaxy Z Fold 7 durable enough for daily use?
Yes. The Galaxy Z Fold 7 uses an Armor Aluminum frame and Corning Gorilla Glass Ceramic 2 on its cover display, both meaningful durability upgrades over previous generations, making it suitable for daily folding without unusual fragility concerns.

Final Verdict

Foldable phones in 2026 have earned a genuine place alongside standard flagships rather than sitting apart as a novelty. The Samsung Galaxy Z Fold 7 remains the most complete book-style option, the Galaxy Z Flip 7 leads for anyone who wants a genuinely compact everyday phone, the Motorola Razr Ultra answers the battery life question directly, and the Honor Magic V6 offers the largest screen and battery combination for buyers who can access it. Whether a foldable is worth it for you comes down to whether the form factor itself, not just the specs, is something you'll actually use every day.
