iPhone 18 Pro Max Review: is it Worth Buying?

Review Team September 16, 2026 14 min read
AppleiPhone 18 Pro MaxiOS 27Phone Reviews

The iPhone 18 Pro Max is Apple's biggest and most powerful traditional iPhone for 2026. It arrives with a new A20 Pro chip, a redesigned camera system with a variable aperture, improved cooling, longer battery life, a smaller Dynamic Island, and iOS 27. This review covers the design, display, cameras, performance, battery, software, and price, so you know exactly what you're getting for $1,299 and up.

Apple has also increased the storage options, with the iPhone 18 Pro Max now available with up to 2TB. The phone keeps the large 6.9-inch OLED display and aluminum unibody design of the previous generation, but several important changes are hidden inside the familiar shell.

The biggest changes show up in three areas: camera control, sustained performance, and battery life. The main camera now has a variable aperture, letting you control how much light enters the lens and how much of the scene stays in focus. The A20 Pro is built on a newer 2nm process and works with a redesigned vapor chamber to hold performance steady during long sessions. Apple also says the Pro Max has its biggest battery life improvement ever on an iPhone.

But specifications only tell part of the story. After testing the phone, publications including Tom's Guide, TechRadar, and WIRED found that the iPhone 18 Pro Max delivers strong performance, excellent cameras, and very good battery life, while also noting that it is heavier and more expensive than its predecessor. So is the iPhone 18 Pro Max worth buying in 2026? Here's a closer look.

Design and Build Quality

At first glance, the iPhone 18 Pro Max does not look dramatically different from the iPhone 17 Pro Max. Apple continues to use an aluminum unibody design, combined with Ceramic Shield 2 on the front and a Ceramic Shield back. The phone is available in four finishes: Black, Silver, Glacier, and Burgundy.

The Burgundy finish is the most distinctive new option, and Glacier gives the phone a noticeably different appearance compared with Apple's traditional color choices. The dimensions are also very similar to the previous generation:

  • Height: 163.4mm
  • Width: 78.0mm
  • Thickness: 8.75mm
  • Weight: 249g

At 249 grams, this is still a heavy smartphone. The large 6.9-inch display, camera hardware, and bigger battery all add to that weight. The phone is also IP68-rated for water and dust resistance, with Apple specifying protection at a maximum depth of 6 meters for up to 30 minutes under laboratory conditions.

A Familiar Design With Important Internal Changes

The biggest design changes are not necessarily visible. Apple has redesigned the thermal system around the A20 Pro chip. The new vapor chamber has three times the surface area of the previous generation, according to Apple, helping the phone maintain performance during demanding workloads.

This matters for anyone who plays demanding games, edits video, or uses the phone for extended stretches. A phone can produce an impressive peak benchmark score for a few minutes, but sustained performance is far more useful when a workload runs for 20, 30, or 60 minutes.

Smaller Dynamic Island

Apple also redesigned the Dynamic Island. It now creates more room for information and can display three Live Activities at once while continuing to support Face ID. It's not a complete removal of the front camera cutout, but Apple made the area smaller and more useful. For everyday users, the Dynamic Island stays handy for timers, calls, music, navigation, sports scores, and other supported activities.

6.9-Inch ProMotion Display

The iPhone 18 Pro Max keeps its large 6.9-inch Super Retina XDR OLED display. It has a resolution of 2868 x 1320 pixels at 460ppi, with ProMotion adaptive refresh rates reaching up to 120Hz. It also supports Always-On display, HDR, True Tone, P3 wide color, and Apple's Dynamic Island.

The display can reach up to 3,000 nits outdoors, according to Apple's specifications, which makes the screen easier to see in bright environments. Apple has also added an anti-reflective coating, useful when using the phone outdoors or near bright lights.

A20 Pro Performance

The A20 Pro is one of the most important upgrades in the iPhone 18 Pro Max. Apple built the chip using a 2nm manufacturing process and redesigned its CPU, GPU, and Neural Engine. The chip includes:

  • 6-core CPU
  • 7-core GPU
  • Neural Accelerators
  • Dual 16-core Neural Engine
  • Hardware-accelerated ray tracing

Apple says the A20 Pro has 50 percent more memory bandwidth than the A19 Pro, while its 7-core GPU can be up to 40 percent faster than the previous generation. Apple also says the redesigned thermal system enables up to a 40 percent gain in sustained performance. These improvements matter because the iPhone 18 Pro Max is built to handle increasingly demanding AI and graphics workloads.

Real-World Performance

In everyday use, the iPhone 18 Pro Max feels extremely fast. Opening apps, switching between them, editing photos, recording video, browsing the web, and multitasking are all handled easily. The bigger improvement shows up during sustained workloads. Tom's Guide's testing found the iPhone 18 Pro models delivering record-setting performance, while the redesigned thermal system helps hold that performance for longer.

For gaming, this means the A20 Pro isn't just built to post a high benchmark score. Apple has also focused on stopping the chip from slowing down as quickly under prolonged loads.

iPhone 18 Pro Max Camera System

The camera system is arguably the biggest reason to choose the iPhone 18 Pro Max over a regular iPhone. Apple has equipped it with three 48MP rear cameras: a 48MP Fusion Main, a 48MP Fusion Ultra Wide, and a 48MP Fusion Telephoto.

What Does the Variable Aperture Do?

The main camera is the most interesting part of the system because it now has a variable aperture, switching between four settings: f/1.48, f/1.8, f/2.8, and f/4.0. This gives the camera more control over light and depth of field. A wider aperture lets in more light and can create a stronger background blur, while a narrower aperture keeps more of the scene in focus. You can now control this directly through the Camera app's Pro controls, something photographers have traditionally expected from dedicated cameras rather than smartphones.

48MP Main Camera

The 48MP Fusion Main camera has a 24mm equivalent focal length, second-generation sensor-shift optical image stabilization, and 100 percent Focus Pixels. It can capture 24MP and 48MP high-resolution photos, backed by Apple's Photonic Engine, Deep Fusion, Smart HDR 5, Night mode, and Photographic Styles. The combination of high-resolution hardware and Apple's image processing produces detailed photos across a wide range of lighting conditions, and the variable aperture is especially useful for people who want more creative control instead of simply pointing and shooting.

48MP Ultra Wide and 48MP Telephoto

The ultra-wide camera also gets a 48MP sensor, with a 13mm equivalent focal length, f/2.2 aperture, and a 120-degree field of view, useful for landscapes, buildings, groups of people, and close-up macro shots. The telephoto camera uses a 48MP sensor with a 100mm equivalent focal length and f/2.8 aperture. Its tetraprism design provides 4x optical zoom, while Apple also offers an 8x optical-quality mode, with digital zoom reaching up to 40x, useful for sports, concerts, travel, and wildlife photography.

New Pro Camera Controls

Apple expanded the Camera app with manual controls for aperture, shutter speed, white balance, histogram, and lens selection, making the iPhone 18 Pro Max more flexible for photographers who want to shape the image instead of relying entirely on automatic processing. Reviews have highlighted the variable-aperture system and these new manual controls as some of the most meaningful camera changes in this generation.

Video Recording

The iPhone 18 Pro Max remains a strong device for mobile video, supporting 4K Dolby Vision, ProRes, ProRes RAW, Apple Log 2, Cinematic mode, spatial video, advanced stabilization, slow motion, Audio Mix, and smart focus tracking. The front camera also supports 4K Dolby Vision recording up to 60fps. For creators shooting YouTube videos, social content, travel footage, or professional work, the combination of camera hardware and Apple's video processing remains a major advantage.

18MP Center Stage Front Camera

The front camera has also been upgraded to an 18MP Center Stage sensor with autofocus, Focus Pixels, improved low-light video, and Center Stage support for photos and video calls, which keeps the subject positioned within the frame during supported calls. It also supports 4K Dolby Vision recording, Cinematic mode, and Apple's computational photography features.

Battery Life and Charging

Battery life is one of the biggest upgrades on the iPhone 18 Pro Max. Apple rates the phone for up to 30 hours of typical use, up to 45 hours of video playback, and up to 40 hours of streamed video playback, figures confirmed on Apple's official technical specifications page. Apple also says the phone can reach 50 percent charge in around 15 minutes with a 60W-capable adapter and the required USB-C cable, and MagSafe and Qi2 wireless charging can reach 25W.

Real-World Battery Testing

Independent testing gives a more practical picture. Tom's Guide measured the iPhone 18 Pro Max at 18 hours and 27 minutes in its battery test, making it the longest-lasting iPhone the publication had tested. TechRadar measured the same 18 hours and 27 minutes, though its comparison showed the improvement over the previous Pro Max was more modest than Apple's headline claims suggest.

The difference comes down to testing methods. Apple's battery claims use specific playback and usage conditions, while independent tests run a controlled workload continuously. The takeaway is that the iPhone 18 Pro Max has excellent endurance, but your actual result will depend on screen brightness, network conditions, gaming, camera use, and streaming habits.

Editor's Note Apple's battery figures and third-party lab results use different testing conditions, so they will rarely match exactly. Treat Apple's numbers as a best-case ceiling and independent test results as a more realistic day-to-day baseline.

iOS 27 and Apple Intelligence

The iPhone 18 Pro Max launches with iOS 27, which introduces Apple's next generation of Apple Intelligence and a significantly improved Siri AI experience. Apple says Siri is now more personal and conversational, and able to use personal context while maintaining Apple's privacy approach. The A20 Pro's dual 16-core Neural Engine is built specifically for this kind of on-device AI processing, which matters because Apple is shifting more processing directly onto the phone rather than relying entirely on cloud servers.

The iPhone 18 Pro Max also includes AI-powered photography features that help with editing, reframing, and other image adjustments. For a full breakdown of everything new in the update, see our iOS 27: Every New Feature Explained guide.

Connectivity

The iPhone 18 Pro Max gets several important connectivity upgrades. It includes Apple's C2 cellular modem, which Apple says improves cellular quality and reliability while using 15 percent less energy than the previous C1X system. The phone also includes:

  • 5G
  • Wi-Fi 7
  • Bluetooth 6
  • Thread
  • Second-generation Ultra Wideband
  • NFC
  • USB-C with DisplayPort and USB 3 speeds up to 10Gbps

Apple's N1 wireless networking chip handles Wi-Fi 7, Bluetooth 6, and Thread connectivity in a single component.

Storage Options and Price

The iPhone 18 Pro Max is available in four storage configurations: 256GB, 512GB, 1TB, and 2TB. The U.S. starting price is $1,299 for 256GB. Apple's official pricing lists the lineup as follows:

The 2TB option is particularly interesting for professional photographers and videographers who regularly record large ProRes or ProRes RAW files, but the price becomes extremely high at the top storage tier.

The camera's variable aperture is one of the clearest hardware differences, and the A20 Pro's new cooling system also matters for anyone running demanding tasks. Battery life has improved as well, although independent testing suggests the real-world increase is smaller than Apple's headline playback figures imply.

Is the iPhone 18 Pro Max Good for Gaming?

Yes. The A20 Pro is built for demanding graphics workloads, and its 7-core GPU includes hardware-accelerated ray tracing. The redesigned vapor chamber matters here too, since gaming creates sustained heat, and Apple claims up to a 40 percent improvement in sustained performance compared with the previous generation. The large display and strong speakers also make the phone enjoyable for games, though the 249g weight can become noticeable during long handheld sessions.

Is the iPhone 18 Pro Max Good for Photography?

It's particularly well suited to photographers who want more manual control. The variable aperture is the standout new feature, letting the main camera adjust between four aperture settings instead of using the same fixed setting in every situation. Combined with the 48MP ultra-wide and 48MP telephoto cameras, the Pro Max offers a genuinely versatile three-camera setup. Casual users can still rely on Apple's automatic processing, while enthusiasts and creators get real flexibility through the new Pro controls.

Is the iPhone 18 Pro Max Worth Upgrading To?

It depends heavily on your current iPhone. If you're coming from an older model, the combination of the A20 Pro chip, improved camera system, longer battery life, brighter display, faster charging, and iOS 27 represents a substantial upgrade. If you already own an iPhone 17 Pro Max, the situation is different. The iPhone 18 Pro Max is more powerful and adds meaningful camera controls, but the basic design, display size, and overall experience remain familiar. TechRadar and WIRED both describe the upgrade as a refinement rather than a complete redesign in their reviews, while still highlighting the camera, battery, and performance gains. iPhone 17 Pro Max owners should weigh the new camera controls and A20 Pro performance carefully before deciding to upgrade.

Conclusion

The iPhone 18 Pro Max is a major refinement of Apple's large Pro iPhone rather than a completely different device. Its biggest improvements are easy to identify: the A20 Pro provides another large jump in processing power, the new vapor chamber helps the phone hold that performance during demanding workloads, and the camera system gets a meaningful upgrade through the variable-aperture 48MP main camera and new Pro controls. Battery life is also improved, and charging is faster than before.

The 6.9-inch ProMotion OLED remains one of the phone's strongest features, while the addition of Wi-Fi 7, Bluetooth 6, the C2 modem, and up to 2TB of storage keeps the phone competitive with other 2026 flagships. There are still compromises: the phone is heavy at 249 grams, the design remains familiar, and the $1,299 starting price makes it an expensive purchase, especially at the 2TB tier.

The upgrade question is different for different users. Someone coming from an older iPhone will find plenty of reasons to upgrade. Someone already using an iPhone 17 Pro Max has fewer reasons to rush, since many of the improvements are refinements rather than fundamental changes. For photographers, creators, gamers, and anyone who wants maximum iPhone performance and battery life, the iPhone 18 Pro Max brings together Apple's latest hardware and software in one device. It isn't simply about having the biggest screen or the fastest chip — the combination of the A20 Pro, variable-aperture camera, improved cooling, longer battery life, Pro controls, iOS 27, and 2TB storage option makes the iPhone 18 Pro Max one of the most capable traditional smartphones available in 2026.
