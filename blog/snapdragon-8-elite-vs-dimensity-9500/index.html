Snapdragon 8 Elite vs Dimensity 9500: Which Chip Is Better?

Tech Editor August 5, 2026 · Updated September 15, 2026 13 min read
QualcommMediaTekChipsetsComparisons

The flagship Android processor market has become more competitive than ever. Qualcomm's Snapdragon chips have traditionally been among the most popular choices for high-end Android phones, while MediaTek has continued closing the gap with its Dimensity family. Two chips that are especially interesting to compare are the Snapdragon 8 Elite and the MediaTek Dimensity 9500.

The Snapdragon 8 Elite arrived as Qualcomm's flagship mobile platform for the 2025 Android flagship generation, introducing Qualcomm's custom Oryon CPU architecture, a redesigned Adreno GPU, a powerful Hexagon NPU, and a 3nm manufacturing process. The Dimensity 9500 followed with MediaTek's third-generation all-big-core CPU design, a new Arm Mali-G1 Ultra GPU, NPU 990, 4-lane UFS 4.1 support, and TSMC's N3P 3nm process.

So, Snapdragon 8 Elite vs Dimensity 9500: which chip is better? The answer depends on what you care about. Benchmark results from currently tested devices show the Dimensity 9500 can outperform the original Snapdragon 8 Elite in several CPU, AnTuTu, and GPU measurements. However, Snapdragon still has important advantages in areas like its modem platform, software and game optimization support, and the wider Snapdragon ecosystem. Let's compare both chips in detail.

The two platforms take very different approaches to achieving flagship performance.

Snapdragon 8 Elite: Qualcomm's Oryon Approach

The Snapdragon 8 Elite represented a major change for Qualcomm. Instead of continuing with the traditional Snapdragon CPU naming structure, Qualcomm brought its custom Oryon CPU architecture to smartphones for the first time. It uses second-generation Oryon CPU cores with speeds of up to 4.32GHz, alongside a redesigned Adreno GPU and an upgraded Hexagon NPU for on-device AI.

Qualcomm claimed up to 45% higher CPU performance and 44% better CPU power efficiency compared with its previous generation, while the Adreno GPU was claimed to deliver up to 40% better performance and 40% better power efficiency. That made the Snapdragon 8 Elite an important processor for flagship Android phones.

Snapdragon 8 Elite CPU

The Snapdragon 8 Elite uses an unusual CPU arrangement compared with many traditional Arm smartphone processors: 2 Oryon prime CPU cores and 6 Oryon performance CPU cores, clocked up to 4.32GHz, with a large shared cache and 64-bit architecture. The custom Oryon architecture lets Qualcomm push very high CPU performance while maintaining strong efficiency, particularly useful for demanding applications, multitasking, emulation, gaming, and other CPU-heavy workloads.

Dimensity 9500: MediaTek Goes All-Big-Core

MediaTek took a different approach with the Dimensity 9500. Instead of mixing traditional small efficiency cores with larger performance cores, MediaTek uses a third-generation all-big-core CPU design: one Arm C1-Ultra core at 4.21GHz, three Arm C1-Premium cores at 3.5GHz, and four Arm C1-Pro cores at 2.7GHz, backed by 16MB of L3 cache and 10MB of system-level cache.

MediaTek says the Dimensity 9500 delivers up to 32% faster single-core performance and 17% faster multi-core performance compared with the previous Dimensity 9400, along with up to 37% lower CPU peak power use and up to 55% better peak power efficiency for the Ultra core. The chip is manufactured using TSMC's N3P 3nm process, giving it a strong focus on both performance and efficiency.

Snapdragon 8 Elite vs Dimensity 9500: CPU Performance

CPU performance is one of the most interesting parts of this comparison. The Snapdragon 8 Elite was already extremely powerful at launch, but the newer Dimensity 9500 has produced higher scores in several independent comparisons since. One widely cited comparison using a vivo X300 Pro (Dimensity 9500) against a Xiaomi 15 (Snapdragon 8 Elite) recorded the following Geekbench 6 results:

BenchmarkDimensity 9500Snapdragon 8 Elite
Geekbench 6 Single-Core3,4523,047
Geekbench 6 Multi-Core10,1289,286

That comparison reported roughly 13% higher single-core and 9% higher multi-core scores for the Dimensity 9500, broadly in line with other outlets' testing, which has generally put the Dimensity 9500 somewhere between about 5% and 13% ahead in Geekbench depending on the specific devices and firmware compared. This is important because benchmark numbers don't always translate directly into a better phone: cooling, software optimization, RAM configuration, firmware, and thermal limits can all affect real performance. Still, based on available testing, the Dimensity 9500 has a consistent, if sometimes modest, advantage in CPU benchmarks over the original Snapdragon 8 Elite.

AnTuTu Performance

AnTuTu combines several areas of smartphone performance, including CPU, GPU, memory, and user experience. In multiple comparisons, the Dimensity 9500 has scored roughly 20-22% higher than the Snapdragon 8 Elite on AnTuTu v11, with the GPU portion of the test showing a particularly large gap, sometimes over 25% higher for the Dimensity 9500. However, benchmark databases combine results from different devices, firmware versions, and cooling systems, so these figures should be treated as reference points rather than universal scores that apply to every phone using each chip.

The key takeaway is that the Dimensity 9500 isn't simply a cheaper alternative to Qualcomm's chip; it's a genuine flagship platform capable of competing directly with the Snapdragon 8 Elite.

GPU and Gaming Performance

Gaming is where the comparison becomes more complicated. The Snapdragon 8 Elite uses Qualcomm's Adreno GPU, while the Dimensity 9500 uses the Arm Mali-G1 Ultra MC12 GPU. Both are designed for high-end Android gaming, but their graphics architectures are different.

Snapdragon 8 Elite Gaming

The Snapdragon 8 Elite supports Qualcomm's Snapdragon Elite Gaming features and Unreal Engine technologies. Qualcomm designed its Adreno GPU to deliver high graphics performance while improving power efficiency, claiming up to 40% better GPU performance and 40% improved GPU power efficiency over its previous generation. Snapdragon also has a major advantage in game-developer familiarity, since many Android games have historically been heavily optimized for Snapdragon hardware, especially popular titles and emulators. That doesn't mean every game runs better on Snapdragon, but it can influence real-world gaming performance.

Dimensity 9500 Gaming

MediaTek has pushed gaming much harder with the Dimensity 9500. Its Mali-G1 Ultra GPU supports hardware-accelerated ray tracing, the Vulkan Raytracing Pipeline, Unreal Engine 5.5+ with Nanite and MegaLights, 120fps ray-traced mobile gaming, MediaTek HyperEngine, and MRFC 3.0 frame-rate enhancement. MediaTek claims up to 33% better GPU performance and up to 119% better ray-tracing performance compared with the Dimensity 9400, with peak GPU power efficiency improving by up to 42%. In available benchmark comparisons against the original Snapdragon 8 Elite, the Dimensity 9500 has produced higher scores in several GPU tests, though actual gaming performance can still depend heavily on the specific phone, and a phone with better cooling can maintain high performance longer than another phone using the same chipset.

Which Is Better for Gaming?

There's no single answer based only on the processor names. The Dimensity 9500 has impressive graphics specifications and has shown strong results in synthetic GPU benchmarks. The Snapdragon 8 Elite benefits from Qualcomm's mature Adreno ecosystem, Snapdragon gaming optimizations, and broad developer support. For games heavily optimized for Snapdragon, the Snapdragon 8 Elite can remain extremely competitive, while for newer graphics technologies and certain benchmark workloads, the Dimensity 9500 can have an advantage. The phone itself matters just as much as the chip.

AI Performance

Artificial intelligence has become one of the biggest areas of competition between Qualcomm and MediaTek. The Snapdragon 8 Elite uses Qualcomm's Hexagon NPU as part of the Qualcomm AI Engine, with Qualcomm claiming up to 45% improved AI performance and 45% better performance per watt compared with the previous generation, plus support for multimodal generative AI and on-device personalization.

The Dimensity 9500 uses MediaTek's NPU 990, which MediaTek says is more than twice as powerful as its previous-generation NPU, with over 2x faster token generation and up to 56% lower peak power use, alongside support for generative and agentic AI workloads including 4K text-to-image generation. The Dimensity 9500 also introduces a super-efficient compute-in-memory NPU design, letting MediaTek target increasingly demanding AI workloads without relying entirely on the CPU or GPU.

AI winner depends on the task

For basic AI features, both processors are more than powerful enough. The difference becomes more important when smartphone manufacturers build their own AI features around the platform: Qualcomm has a large ecosystem of AI tools and developer support, while MediaTek has focused heavily on generative and agentic AI processing in the Dimensity 9500. The software experience on the individual phone can matter more than the raw NPU specifications.

Snapdragon 8 Elite vs Dimensity 9500: Power Efficiency

Both processors use a TSMC 3nm manufacturing process, but the Dimensity 9500 uses the newer N3P process while the Snapdragon 8 Elite uses N3E. MediaTek has also redesigned its CPU and cache architecture around power efficiency, claiming up to 37% lower CPU peak power use compared with the Dimensity 9400 and up to 30% better power efficiency in certain multitasking workloads. Qualcomm also focused heavily on efficiency with the Snapdragon 8 Elite, claiming 44% greater CPU power efficiency and 40% better GPU power efficiency compared with the previous Snapdragon generation.

The result is that both chips are highly efficient by flagship standards. However, because smartphone battery life depends on the complete device, it's not accurate to say either chipset will automatically give every phone longer battery life; battery capacity, display resolution, refresh rate, modem activity, cooling, and software all matter.

Connectivity Comparison

Qualcomm has traditionally had an advantage in modem technology, and the Snapdragon 8 Elite continues that trend. It uses Qualcomm's X80 5G Modem-RF System, supporting 5G Advanced features and peak download speeds of up to 10Gbps under Qualcomm's specified conditions, plus Wi-Fi 7 through FastConnect 7900.

The Dimensity 9500 supports 3GPP Release 17, 5G carrier aggregation, 5G/4G Dual SIM Dual Active, and up to 7.4Gbps downlink on supported Sub-6GHz configurations, along with Wi-Fi 7 at a peak data rate of 7.3Gbps and Bluetooth 6.0. For normal smartphone use, both platforms offer more than enough connectivity performance, but Qualcomm's modem ecosystem remains an important advantage for manufacturers targeting a wide range of global networks.

Camera and Video Capabilities

Both processors are designed to power flagship camera systems. The Snapdragon 8 Elite uses Qualcomm's Spectra ISP and supports advanced computational photography features, AI-powered image processing, and high-end video capabilities, including real-time semantic segmentation and AI-enhanced video processing.

The Dimensity 9500 uses MediaTek's Imagiq 1190 imaging platform, supporting up to 320MP camera sensors, 8K60 video, 4K120 video with EIS, 4K60 cinematic video, AI camera processing, and advanced focus and image processing. Again, the chipset is only part of the camera equation; the camera sensor, lenses, ISP tuning, image-processing algorithms, and manufacturer software can make a much larger difference to the final photographs than the chip alone.

Storage and Memory

The Dimensity 9500 has a particularly interesting advantage in storage technology: it's the first MediaTek platform to support 4-lane UFS 4.1, which MediaTek says can provide 100% faster sequential data speeds and up to 40% faster AI large-model loading compared with the previous implementation. The Snapdragon 8 Elite supports LPDDR5X memory and UFS 4.0 storage. For everyday users, the difference may not be obvious, but faster storage can help with large game installations, application loading, file transfers, and AI model loading.

Editor's Note Benchmark comparisons like these are useful, but treat them as a starting point rather than a final verdict. Different labs test different phones with different cooling systems and firmware, so absolute numbers can shift meaningfully between comparisons even for the same two chips.

Which Chip Should You Choose?

Choose Snapdragon 8 Elite if you want:

  • Strong flagship performance with Qualcomm's Oryon CPU
  • Adreno GPU with mature Android gaming optimization
  • Qualcomm's X80 modem for broad global connectivity
  • Strong multimodal AI capabilities
  • A wide selection of flagship phones and strong emulator support

Choose Dimensity 9500 if you want:

  • Newer all-big-core CPU architecture
  • Strong multi-core performance and high AnTuTu scores
  • Mali-G1 Ultra graphics with advanced ray tracing
  • NPU 990 AI processing built for generative and agentic AI
  • 4-lane UFS 4.1, Wi-Fi 7, and Bluetooth 6.0

Which Chip Is More Future-Proof?

The Dimensity 9500 is newer than the original Snapdragon 8 Elite, so it naturally benefits from newer CPU architecture, newer connectivity features, newer storage support, and newer AI capabilities. That said, the Snapdragon 8 Elite still has a long useful life ahead of it. Both are flagship-class processors capable of handling demanding Android games, multitasking, photography, video editing, and AI workloads, and the newer Dimensity 9500 doesn't make the Snapdragon 8 Elite obsolete.

It's also worth knowing that MediaTek has since moved on to the Dimensity 9600 family, including the Dimensity 9600 Pro and Dimensity 9600M, so if you're shopping in late 2026, it's worth checking whether the phone you're considering uses the Dimensity 9500 specifically or one of these newer chips.

People Also Ask

Is Dimensity 9500 better than Snapdragon 8 Elite?
In several independent CPU and AnTuTu benchmarks, the Dimensity 9500 scores higher than the original Snapdragon 8 Elite, roughly 9-13% ahead in Geekbench and around 22% ahead in AnTuTu 11 in some comparisons. The Snapdragon 8 Elite still holds advantages in modem technology, gaming ecosystem maturity, and software optimization support.
Which chip is better for gaming, Snapdragon 8 Elite or Dimensity 9500?
The Dimensity 9500 has stronger GPU benchmark scores and newer ray-tracing features, while the Snapdragon 8 Elite benefits from a more mature gaming optimization ecosystem and broad developer support. Cooling and software optimization on the specific phone often matter more than the chip alone.
Is the Snapdragon 8 Elite the same as the Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5?
No. The Snapdragon 8 Elite (2024) and Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 (2025) are different Qualcomm platforms. The Gen 5 is a newer, separate chip and should not be confused with the original Snapdragon 8 Elite when comparing benchmark results.
Which chip has better AI performance?
Both are strong AI platforms. The Snapdragon 8 Elite's Hexagon NPU focuses on multimodal AI and personalization, while the Dimensity 9500's NPU 990 emphasizes generative and agentic AI with an additional efficient compute-in-memory NPU. The better experience often depends on how the phone manufacturer implements the hardware in software.
Does the Dimensity 9500 have a better modem than the Snapdragon 8 Elite?
Qualcomm's X80 modem is generally considered the stronger overall connectivity platform, with a mature global carrier ecosystem, while the Dimensity 9500's integrated 5G modem still supports fast Sub-6GHz speeds and Wi-Fi 7 for most everyday use.

Final Comparison

The Snapdragon 8 Elite vs Dimensity 9500 comparison is interesting because these two chips represent different generations and design philosophies. The Snapdragon 8 Elite introduced Qualcomm's custom Oryon CPU architecture to smartphones and delivered a major performance and efficiency improvement over the previous Snapdragon generation, with Adreno GPU, Hexagon NPU, and X80 modem making it a complete flagship platform.

The Dimensity 9500 takes a newer approach: its third-generation all-big-core CPU, 3nm N3P manufacturing, Mali-G1 Ultra GPU, NPU 990, UFS 4.1 support, and advanced ray-tracing features make it a serious competitor to Qualcomm's previous flagship. Available benchmark comparisons show the Dimensity 9500 ahead of the original Snapdragon 8 Elite in several CPU and overall performance tests, but that doesn't mean every Dimensity 9500 phone will automatically be faster than every Snapdragon 8 Elite phone. Cooling, RAM, software optimization, battery capacity, display settings, and game optimization can all change the real-world experience.

There's also an important naming issue for buyers in 2026: the Snapdragon 8 Elite and Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 are different processors, and the newer Gen 5 shouldn't be confused with the original Snapdragon 8 Elite when comparing benchmark results. For this specific comparison, the Dimensity 9500 represents a newer and very competitive flagship platform, while the Snapdragon 8 Elite remains a powerful and mature choice with strong gaming, connectivity, and ecosystem advantages. Ultimately, if you're choosing between two phones, compare the complete devices rather than the chipset names alone; the processor is the heart of a smartphone, but the cooling system, battery, display, software, and optimization determine how well that heart performs in the real world.
