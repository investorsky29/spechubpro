Best Budget Smartphone Under $300 in 2026

Mobile Team August 10, 2026 · Updated September 15, 2026 14 min read
SamsungMotorolaXiaomiBuying Guide

Finding a good phone doesn't always mean spending $500, $700, or more. In 2026, the budget smartphone market has improved a lot, and you can now find phones with AMOLED displays, 120Hz refresh rates, 5G, large batteries, capable cameras, and modern software without going over a $300 budget.

However, choosing the best budget smartphone under $300 can still be confusing. Some phones focus on battery life, while others offer better cameras, displays, gaming performance, or software support. A phone that looks impressive on a specification sheet may not be the best choice for everyday use.

In this guide, we look at some of the most interesting budget smartphones under $300 in 2026, including Samsung, Motorola, Xiaomi, POCO, Nothing, and other brands. We'll compare their displays, processors, cameras, batteries, gaming performance, and software support so you can find the phone that fits your needs.

Note: Smartphone prices can change depending on the country, retailer, storage version, and promotions. Some phones may also be sold below $300 in one market but cost more in another.

1. Samsung Galaxy A17 5G

The Samsung Galaxy A17 5G is one of the phones to consider if you want a familiar Samsung experience without spending much money. Samsung launched it at a $199 MSRP in the US, and it's been discounted to around $170 for much of its time on sale; pricing and availability can vary by market.

One of its biggest advantages is the Super AMOLED display. AMOLED screens provide deeper blacks and stronger contrast than traditional LCD panels, making the phone enjoyable for YouTube, Netflix, TikTok, social media, and browsing, even though its 90Hz refresh rate is a step behind some rivals at this price. The phone runs on Samsung's Exynos 1330 chipset, supports 5G, and charges at 25W with no wireless charging. It's also worth knowing the A17 carries an IP54 rating, lower than the IP68 found on some competitors, so it's splash-resistant rather than fully submersible.

For people who plan to keep a phone for several years, software support is another important factor. Samsung promises up to six major OS updates on the A17, putting more emphasis on long-term updates across its newer budget Galaxy phones.

Why consider the Galaxy A17 5G?

  • Super AMOLED display
  • 5G connectivity
  • 5,000mAh battery
  • Samsung software experience with up to six years of OS updates
  • Good option for everyday apps
  • Frequently discounted below its already-affordable MSRP

The Galaxy A17 5G isn't designed to compete with expensive Galaxy S-series phones in raw performance. Instead, it focuses on providing a balanced smartphone experience at a lower price. If you mainly use WhatsApp, Instagram, YouTube, TikTok, Chrome, banking apps, photography, and casual games, its hardware can be enough for everyday use.

2. Motorola Moto G Power 2026

The Motorola Moto G Power 2026 is aimed at users who care about battery life, durability, and a large screen. Motorola's specifications list a 5,200mAh battery, 30W wired charging plus 15W wireless charging, a 6.8-inch FHD+ display with a 120Hh refresh rate, 8GB RAM, 128GB storage, and a MediaTek Dimensity 6300 processor. The phone also has IP68/IP69 protection and MIL-STD-810H certification, a tougher rating than the Galaxy A17's IP54.

That combination makes the Moto G Power 2026 interesting for people who spend a lot of time away from a charger. The 120Hz refresh rate also makes scrolling and supported animations look smoother than on many cheaper 60Hz phones, and the inclusion of wireless charging is a genuine step up from most phones in this price range.

Moto G Power 2026 highlights

  • 6.8-inch FHD+ display
  • 120Hz refresh rate
  • MediaTek Dimensity 6300
  • 8GB RAM
  • 128GB storage
  • 5,200mAh battery
  • 30W wired charging, 15W wireless charging
  • IP68/IP69 protection, MIL-STD-810H certification
  • 3.5mm headphone jack
  • Stereo speakers with Dolby Atmos

Motorola also claims up to two days of battery life under its testing conditions. Real-world battery life will depend on your apps, brightness, mobile network, gaming, and other usage. For someone who wants a tough phone with a big screen and long battery life, this is one of the models worth checking, though at $299.99 it sits right at the top of this budget.

3. CMF Phone 2 Pro

The CMF Phone 2 Pro brings something different to the budget market. It combines an AMOLED display, a versatile camera system, and a relatively clean software experience. Nothing's sub-brand lists a 6.77-inch AMOLED display with a 120Hz refresh rate, a 5,000mAh battery, and a MediaTek Dimensity 7300 Pro processor. The camera system includes a 50MP main camera, a 50MP 2x optical telephoto camera, an 8MP ultra-wide camera, and a 16MP front camera.

That telephoto camera is particularly interesting because dedicated zoom cameras are still uncommon in this price range. The phone also supports 33W wired charging and 4K video recording, making it useful for people who take photos and videos for social media. It carries an IP54 rating, matching the Galaxy A17 but behind the Moto G Power's tougher certification.

CMF Phone 2 Pro highlights

  • 6.77-inch AMOLED display
  • 120Hz refresh rate
  • Dimensity 7300 Pro
  • 5,000mAh battery
  • 33W charging
  • 50MP main camera, 50MP 2x optical telephoto camera
  • 8MP ultra-wide camera
  • 16MP selfie camera
  • 4K video recording

The exact price and availability depend heavily on the market, generally landing around $276 in markets where it's sold, though Nothing's official pricing has varied by region and storage configuration. If you care about display quality and camera versatility, the CMF Phone 2 Pro deserves attention.

4. Redmi Note 15 5G

Xiaomi's Redmi Note series continues to target buyers who want strong specifications without paying flagship prices. The Redmi Note 15 5G comes with a 6.77-inch AMOLED display, 120Hz refresh rate, Snapdragon 6 Gen 3 processor, and a 5,520mAh battery. Xiaomi also gives it a 108MP main camera and 45W charging.

The display is one of its biggest strengths. It has a 2392x1080 resolution and supports up to 120Hz, and Xiaomi lists a peak brightness of up to 3,200 nits under its specified conditions.

Redmi Note 15 5G highlights

  • 6.77-inch AMOLED
  • 120Hz refresh rate
  • Snapdragon 6 Gen 3
  • 108MP main camera, 8MP ultra-wide
  • 20MP front camera
  • 5,520mAh battery
  • 45W charging
  • 5G
  • Up to 512GB storage on some configurations

The 5G version is particularly interesting if you want a combination of battery capacity, display quality, and 5G connectivity. However, availability and pricing vary considerably by region, so check the exact local version before buying.

5. POCO X7

If gaming and performance are more important to you, the POCO X7 deserves a place on your shortlist. The phone uses the MediaTek Dimensity 7300-Ultra chipset and has a 6.67-inch 1.5K AMOLED display with a 120Hz refresh rate. It also has a 50MP main camera with OIS and a 5,110mAh battery. POCO also includes features designed around gaming, including high touch sampling in Game Turbo mode.

POCO X7 highlights

  • Dimensity 7300-Ultra
  • 6.67-inch 1.5K AMOLED display
  • 120Hz refresh rate
  • 50MP main camera with OIS
  • 5,110mAh battery
  • 45W charging
  • IP68 protection
  • Stereo speakers
  • Up to 12GB RAM and 512GB storage

POCO claims an AnTuTu score of more than 700,000 under its internal testing conditions, though benchmark results can vary depending on software, temperature, and testing method. The combination of the Dimensity 7300-Ultra and 120Hz AMOLED display makes the POCO X7 especially interesting for users who play games regularly.

6. Redmi Note 15

If battery life is more important to you than 5G, the standard Redmi Note 15 is another option. The phone has a 6,000mAh battery, 33W charging, a 6.77-inch AMOLED display, and a 120Hz refresh rate. It uses the MediaTek Helio G100-Ultra processor.

The large battery is the biggest attraction here. For people who spend hours watching videos, browsing social media, listening to music, or using messaging apps, having a 6,000mAh battery can be useful.

Redmi Note 15 highlights

  • 6.77-inch AMOLED
  • 120Hz refresh rate
  • MediaTek Helio G100-Ultra
  • 108MP main camera
  • 20MP front camera
  • 6,000mAh battery
  • 33W charging
  • Expandable storage on supported versions

One thing to remember is that the standard Redmi Note 15 is a 4G phone, while the Redmi Note 15 5G uses the Snapdragon 6 Gen 3 and supports 5G.

7. Motorola Moto G 2026

The regular Moto G 2026 is worth considering if you want to spend less than the full $300. Motorola launched it at a $199 MSRP, matching the Galaxy A17 on price, with a 6.7-inch 120Hz LCD display and a 5,000mAh battery, though it comes with a lower RAM allowance (4GB at launch) than some rivals here.

It's designed more for basic smartphone use than demanding gaming, making it suitable for messaging, social media, video streaming, browsing, calls, and other everyday tasks. The Moto G family also tends to focus heavily on battery capacity, clean software, and practical features rather than trying to compete with expensive phones on every specification. For someone who simply needs a reliable smartphone without spending close to $300, the Moto G 2026 can make sense.

Editor's Note The Galaxy A17 5G and Moto G 2026 launch at the same $199 price in the US, which makes them the most direct head-to-head in this guide. The A17 wins on display quality (AMOLED vs LCD) and software support, while the Moto G counters with a faster 120Hz panel and more RAM in some configurations. Check current RAM and storage before buying either, since base configurations can change between markets.

Which Budget Phone Under $300 Is Best for Gaming?

Gaming changes the buying decision. A phone with a 108MP camera doesn't automatically provide better gaming performance. For games such as PUBG Mobile, Call of Duty Mobile, Free Fire, eFootball, or other demanding titles, the processor, GPU, cooling system, display refresh rate, and software optimization are more important.

The POCO X7 is particularly interesting because it uses the Dimensity 7300-Ultra, has a 120Hz AMOLED display, and includes gaming-focused touch features. The Moto G Power 2026 can handle everyday gaming, but its Dimensity 6300 is positioned below the chip used in the POCO X7. The CMF Phone 2 Pro also has a capable Dimensity 7300 Pro platform, making it another phone to consider for moderate gaming.

However, if gaming is your main reason for buying a phone, you should also consider increasing your budget if possible. Phones around $300-$400 can sometimes provide a noticeable jump in sustained gaming performance; see our best gaming phones of 2026 guide for dedicated options at that level.

Which Is the Best Budget Smartphone Under $300 for Camera?

Camera quality isn't only about megapixels. A 200MP or 108MP camera doesn't automatically produce better photos than a 50MP camera. Sensor size, image processing, stabilization, lens quality, and software all matter.

The CMF Phone 2 Pro is interesting because it has both a 50MP main camera and a dedicated 50MP 2x optical telephoto camera. The Redmi Note 15 5G also offers a 108MP main camera and 20MP selfie camera. Meanwhile, the POCO X7 has a 50MP main camera with OIS, which can help stabilize photos and videos. So, when comparing budget smartphones under $300, look beyond the megapixel number.

Which Phone Under $300 Has the Best Battery?

Battery life is one area where budget phones can compete surprisingly well. The Redmi Note 15 has a 6,000mAh battery, while the Redmi Note 15 5G has 5,520mAh. The Moto G Power 2026 has a 5,200mAh battery, and the POCO X7 has a 5,110mAh battery.

However, battery capacity is only part of the story. A phone with a more efficient processor, optimized software, and lower display power consumption can sometimes last longer than another phone with a larger battery. Your usage also matters: gaming, 5G, high brightness, GPS, and video recording can drain a battery much faster.

What Should You Look for in a Phone Under $300?

Before buying, don't focus on only one specification.

1. Processor

The processor controls much of the phone's performance. If you use demanding apps or games, look for a stronger chipset such as the Dimensity 7300 series or Snapdragon 6 Gen 3 rather than choosing a phone based only on camera megapixels.

2. Display

A 120Hz display can make scrolling and animations feel smoother. AMOLED is also a major advantage because it offers deeper blacks and strong contrast, though the Galaxy A17's 90Hz AMOLED panel shows that refresh rate and panel type don't always come as a package deal.

3. Battery

A battery between 5,000mAh and 6,000mAh is common in this category. But remember that charging speed and processor efficiency matter too.

4. Software Updates

Software support is easy to overlook when buying a cheap phone. A phone that receives updates for several years can be a better long-term purchase than one with slightly better hardware but limited support.

5. Storage

Try to get at least 128GB if possible. If you install many games, record lots of videos, or download movies and music, 256GB can be much more comfortable.

6. 5G

5G is useful if your mobile network provides good 5G coverage in your area. However, don't sacrifice everything else simply to get 5G. A phone with better battery life, software support, or a better display may still be more useful for your needs.

Are Budget Smartphones Good Enough in 2026?

Yes, for many people. The biggest difference between a $300 phone and a $1,000 flagship is no longer simply whether the phone can perform basic tasks. Modern budget phones can browse the web, stream high-quality video, run social media apps, take good photos, use 5G, play games, and handle everyday multitasking.

The compromises usually become more obvious with demanding gaming, advanced photography, video recording, wireless charging, premium materials, flagship processors, and long-term performance. That means you should spend your money based on what you actually use.

Should You Buy an Older Flagship Instead?

Sometimes an older flagship can be attractive when its price falls below $300. You may get a better camera system, faster processor, wireless charging, or a more premium build.

However, there are risks. An older flagship may have a smaller battery, fewer remaining software updates, an older battery that has degraded, no warranty, higher repair costs, or used or refurbished condition. A new budget phone may therefore be the safer choice for someone who wants a fresh battery and warranty. If you find an older flagship under $300, check its condition, software support, battery health, and warranty before buying.

People Also Ask

What is the best budget smartphone under $300 in 2026?
There is no single phone that is ideal for every user. The Galaxy A17 5G focuses on Samsung's software experience and AMOLED display, the Moto G Power 2026 focuses on battery life and durability, the POCO X7 focuses more on performance and gaming, while the Redmi Note 15 models emphasize displays and battery capacity.
Is $300 enough to buy a good smartphone?
Yes. A $300 budget can get you a phone with features such as a 120Hz display, large battery, 5G, AMOLED technology, and capable cameras, depending on the market and current price.
Which Samsung phone is under $300?
The Galaxy A17 5G launched at a $199 MSRP in the US and is commonly found discounted further. Pricing can vary by country and storage configuration.
Are phones under $300 good for gaming?
Yes, but they are better suited to casual and moderate gaming than flagship-level gaming. Look for a capable processor, good cooling, a high-refresh-rate display, and sufficient RAM.
Is 5G worth it on a budget phone?
It can be worthwhile if your network provides reliable 5G coverage. However, battery life, processor performance, software support, and display quality should also be considered.
Can a $300 phone last four years?
It can, but long-term use depends heavily on software support, battery health, build quality, and how demanding your usage is. A phone with longer update support can be a better long-term purchase.
Should I buy a 4G or 5G phone under $300?
If 5G coverage is strong where you live and you plan to keep the phone for several years, 5G can be useful. If your network is mainly 4G, you may get better value by choosing a phone with stronger battery life, display, or performance instead.

Final Thoughts

The best budget smartphone under $300 doesn't have to be the phone with the biggest camera or the fastest processor. Different phones in this price range have different strengths. The Samsung Galaxy A17 5G is worth considering if you want Samsung's ecosystem, an AMOLED display, 5G, and an affordable price around $199. The Moto G Power 2026 is focused on battery life, durability, a large 120Hz display, and practical features like wireless charging.

The CMF Phone 2 Pro stands out with its AMOLED display and versatile camera system, including a dedicated telephoto camera. The Redmi Note 15 5G combines a 120Hz AMOLED display, Snapdragon 6 Gen 3, 108MP camera, and 5,520mAh battery. The POCO X7 is worth looking at if performance and gaming are higher on your list, thanks to its Dimensity 7300-Ultra processor, 120Hz 1.5K AMOLED display, and gaming-focused touch features. Finally, the standard Redmi Note 15 is an interesting option if battery capacity is one of your biggest priorities, with its 6,000mAh battery and 120Hz AMOLED display.

Before buying, always check the current price in your country, storage version, network compatibility, warranty, and software support. Prices can change quickly, especially in the budget phone market. A $300 budget is enough to get a capable smartphone in 2026. The important part is choosing the phone whose strengths match the way you actually use your device.
