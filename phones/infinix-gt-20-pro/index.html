Editorial Overview

This is an editorial analysis of the specifications listed on this page. It is not based on SpecHub lab testing, so it makes no claims about benchmarks, battery life or camera results. Manufacturer lab figures are not reproduced here.

Phone Overview

The Infinix GT 20 Pro is a gaming-oriented phone released on April 26, 2024, with a Dimensity 8200 Ultimate chip, a 144Hz AMOLED display and an RGB-lit back panel. It is the successor to the GT 10 Pro. It is aimed at buyers who want gaming performance at a mid-range price.

Performance and Processor

The phone uses the MediaTek Dimensity 8200 Ultimate (4 nm) with a CPU led by one core at up to 3.1 GHz and a Mali-G610 MC6 GPU. RAM is 8GB or 12GB and storage is 256GB (UFS 3.1). SpecHub has not benchmarked the phone.

Display Experience

The 6.78-inch AMOLED display has 1080 x 2436 pixels (about 388 ppi) and a 144Hz refresh rate, with 2304Hz PWM dimming and a peak brightness of up to 1,300 nits.

Camera Experience

The rear cameras are a 108MP main (f/1.8, 1/1.67-inch sensor, optical stabilisation), a 2MP macro and a 2MP depth sensor. SpecHub has not tested image quality, and front-camera details are omitted because they were not verified.

Battery and Charging

The battery is 5000 mAh with 45W wired charging and reverse charging. Wireless charging is not listed. We have no endurance or charge-time tests.

Software and Features

The phone launched with Android 14 and XOS 14. It supports 5G (SA and NSA), has an optical in-screen fingerprint sensor and is rated IP54. The back has customizable RGB LEDs.

Who Should Consider This Phone?

Mobile gamers who want a powerful chip, a very smooth screen and distinctive styling at a mid-range price will find it a good fit.

Who May Prefer Another Phone?

People who want faster charging, wireless charging or a stronger camera should look at a more expensive phone, such as the Infinix GT 30 Pro.

Editorial Verdict

The GT 20 Pro offers strong gaming hardware and a smooth display at a modest price. Its moderate charging speed and basic secondary cameras are the main trade-offs.
