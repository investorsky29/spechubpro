Editorial Overview

This is an editorial analysis of the specifications listed on this page. It is not based on SpecHub lab testing, so it makes no claims about benchmarks, battery life or camera results.

Phone Overview

The RedMagic 11 Pro is the first phone in this group built on the Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5, and SpecHub lists it as a November 2025 release at $699.

It follows the usual RedMagic formula: a large 144Hz AMOLED screen with an under-display selfie camera, active cooling, shoulder triggers and a big battery. It is aimed at gamers who want the newest chip and Android version without moving up to the 11S Pro.

Performance and Processor

The Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 is paired with an Adreno 840 GPU, and the CPU is listed at up to 4.6GHz. That is below the 4.74GHz listed for the 11S Pro's Leading Version chip, so the 11 Pro appears to be the standard-clock variant. SpecHub has no benchmark data to say how much difference this makes in practice.

The page describes liquid cooling with an integrated fan, which is intended to help the phone keep its speed during long sessions. The fan speed is not listed. RAM comes in 12GB, 16GB or 24GB and storage in 256GB, 512GB or 1TB. There is no memory card slot.

Display Experience

The screen is a 6.85-inch AMOLED at 1216 x 2688 pixels (about 430 pixels per inch) with a 144Hz refresh rate. These figures are the same as on the 10-series models listed on SpecHub, so the move to the 11 Pro is not about the display.

A 144Hz screen only shows its full smoothness in games and apps that run at that rate. The 16MP selfie camera is under the display, which removes any cut-out, with the usual trade-off that selfie quality can be lower than on a conventional front camera.

Camera Experience

The camera list matches earlier RedMagic models: a 50MP main with OIS, a 50MP ultrawide and a 2MP macro, with no telephoto lens. Camera quality is not the priority here, which fits a phone whose design effort is clearly going into cooling and controls.

We have not tested its photos or video, so this page does not rate image quality. For casual snapshots the hardware is adequate on paper, but a photography-first buyer should look at independent samples.

Battery and Charging

The 11 Pro lists a 7500mAh battery, up from 7050mAh in the 10S Pro and 10 Pro+. Wired charging is 80W, which is lower than the 120W listed for the 10 Pro+ and 10S Pro+. The specification table lists wireless charging as not supported, but RedMagic's own product page describes wireless fast charging for the 11 Pro, so treat that table entry as unconfirmed and check before buying.

The practical trade-off is a larger battery with moderate charging speed. SpecHub has no battery or charge-time test results, so we cannot say how long it lasts.

Software and Features

The phone runs Android 16 with RedMagic OS 11, which is the newest Android version listed in this group of pages, shared with the 11S Pro. RedMagic's store page states that the 11 Pro gets two major Android updates and three years of security updates from launch, though policies can differ by region.

It supports 5G on Sub-6GHz bands, Wi-Fi 7, Bluetooth 5.4, NFC and USB-C 3.2 Gen 2, with dual Nano-SIM. RedMagic's product page also lists a 3.5mm headphone jack and 520Hz shoulder triggers, and states IPX8 water resistance (tested under lab conditions). None of these appear in this page's specification table.

Who Should Consider This Phone?

Gamers who want the Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 and Android 16 at the lowest listed price among the Gen 5 RedMagic models will find it a fit. It also suits people who prefer a wired headphone connection and want a larger battery than the 10-series offered.

Who May Prefer Another Phone?

Buyers who want wireless charging should confirm the 11 Pro's status first; the 11S Pro's table lists it explicitly. Those who want the fastest wired charging may prefer a 120W model from the 10 series. Camera-focused buyers should look at more mainstream flagships.

Editorial Verdict

The 11 Pro offers the newest platform in this group at a price that matches the older 10S Pro. Its strengths are the Gen 5 chip, Android 16, a 7500mAh battery and the usual gaming features. Its weaknesses are moderate 80W charging and a camera setup that is not a priority. It is a good choice for gamers who care more about hardware than photos.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which Android version does the RedMagic 11 Pro run, and for how long will it be updated?
The specification table lists Android 16 with RedMagic OS 11. RedMagic's store page states two major Android updates and three years of security updates from launch. Check the policy for your region.
How is the 11 Pro different from the 11S Pro?
The 11 Pro lists the Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 with the CPU at up to 4.6GHz, while the 11S Pro lists a Leading Version at up to 4.74GHz. The 11S Pro's table also lists 80W wireless charging. The 11 Pro offers up to 24GB of RAM and 1TB of storage, against 16GB and 512GB on the 11S Pro.
Does the RedMagic 11 Pro have an IP water-resistance rating?
RedMagic's product page states IPX8 water resistance, tested under lab conditions. This page's specification table has no IP rating row, so check the manufacturer's current specification for your region.
Is the 11 Pro a big upgrade over the 10 series?
On paper, the screen and camera hardware are unchanged. The changes are the Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 chip, Android 16 and a 7500mAh battery (the 10S Pro and 10 Pro+ list 7050mAh). Charging is 80W, lower than the 120W on the 10 Pro+.
Does it support wireless charging?
This page's table lists wireless charging as not supported, but RedMagic's product page describes wireless fast charging for the 11 Pro. Treat the table entry as unconfirmed and check before buying.
Is it a good camera phone?
It is not designed as one. The listed hardware is a 50MP main with OIS, a 50MP ultrawide and a 2MP macro, and camera quality is not the focus of this gaming phone.
What are the listed price and release date?
SpecHub lists the RedMagic 11 Pro at $699 and a November 2025 release. Launch coverage reported $699 in some international markets and $749 in the US, and prices vary by region, memory configuration and retailer.
