Editorial Overview

This is an editorial analysis of the specifications listed on this page. It is not based on SpecHub lab testing, so it makes no claims about benchmarks, battery life or camera results.

Phone Overview

The RedMagic 10S Pro is the standard model in the 10S line, listed at $699 on this page. It is built around mobile gaming rather than all-round camera performance: a 6.85-inch 144Hz AMOLED screen with the selfie camera placed under the display, a cooling system with an internal fan, and physical shoulder triggers.

It is aimed at people who play demanding games for long stretches and want hardware that is designed for that job, without paying for the larger battery and faster charging of the Pro+ version.

Performance and Processor

The phone uses the Snapdragon 8 Elite Leading Version with an Adreno 830 GPU. The spec table lists the CPU at up to 4.47GHz, and RedMagic markets the chip as an overclocked version of the Snapdragon 8 Elite. RedMagic also pairs it with a separate RedCore R3 Pro gaming chip, which is not part of this page's specification table.

For gaming, the more useful question is how long a phone holds its speed once it warms up. This is what the ICE-X cooling system and its 23,000 RPM fan are meant to address. SpecHub has not benchmarked the phone, so we do not quote frame rates or temperatures. RAM is listed as 12GB, 16GB or 24GB and storage as 256GB, 512GB or 1TB. There is no card slot, so the storage you choose at purchase is final.

Display Experience

The 6.85-inch AMOLED panel has a resolution of 1216 x 2688 pixels, which works out to roughly 430 pixels per inch. That is sharp enough for text and video, and the lower pixel count compared with a QHD panel means the GPU has fewer pixels to draw in each frame of a game.

The 144Hz refresh rate makes motion look smoother in games that can run at high frame rates, but many titles cap their frame rate lower, so the benefit depends on what you play. Because the 16MP front camera sits under the display, the screen has no notch or punch-hole in the way during gameplay. Under-display selfie cameras commonly trade some image quality for that clean look, so heavy selfie users may want to check sample photos.

Camera Experience

The rear system lists a 50MP main camera with optical image stabilization, a 50MP ultrawide and a 2MP macro camera. No telephoto lens is listed, so distant subjects would rely on digital cropping. The 2MP macro sensor has a low resolution and is best seen as an extra rather than a main reason to buy.

The page itself notes that the camera system is a lower priority than gaming. SpecHub has not tested image or video quality, so we cannot comment on colour, dynamic range or low-light results. If photography matters to you, look at independent camera samples before deciding.

Battery and Charging

The 7050mAh battery is large for a phone with a 6.85-inch screen, and it is paired with 80W wired charging. There is no wireless charging. Within the RedMagic range on SpecHub, the 10 Pro+ lists the same 7050mAh capacity with 120W charging, so the 10S Pro trades faster charging for its lower listed price.

We have no battery endurance or charge-time tests for this phone. The listed numbers tell you the capacity and the maximum charging power, not how many hours of gaming you will get.

Software and Features

The phone ships with Android 15 and RedMagic OS 10.5, a gaming-oriented version of Android. This page does not itemise its gaming features, so we do not describe them in more detail here.

Connectivity includes 5G (with SA and NSA listed), Wi-Fi 7, Bluetooth 5.4, NFC and a USB-C 3.2 Gen 2 port, with dual Nano-SIM support. Wi-Fi 7 only helps if your router supports it. The 520Hz shoulder triggers are the feature most likely to separate it from a standard flagship. The specification table has no audio-jack or IP-rating row, so confirm those with the retailer if they matter to you.

Who Should Consider This Phone?

Players of competitive or long-session games who want fan cooling and physical triggers, and who do not need the fastest charging, are the main audience. It also suits anyone who wants a large screen and a big battery without paying for the Pro+ model's extras.

Who May Prefer Another Phone?

Photography-focused buyers, anyone who wants wireless charging, and people who find a 229 g phone too heavy should look elsewhere. If fast top-ups matter, the 120W models in this range are a better fit. SpecHub also lists the newer RedMagic 11 Pro, which has a newer chipset and a 7500mAh battery, so compare the two before buying. Prices vary by region and retailer.

Editorial Verdict

The 10S Pro is a focused gaming phone that keeps the main hardware features of the 10S line (high-clocked chip, active cooling, 144Hz AMOLED screen and shoulder triggers) at a lower listed price than the Pro+. Its main compromises are 80W charging, an unremarkable listed camera setup and no wireless charging. It is a sensible pick for dedicated gamers on a tighter budget, and a poor fit for people who want a balanced camera phone.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does the RedMagic 10S Pro differ from the 10S Pro+?
The two share the same chipset, 6.85-inch 144Hz display and listed camera hardware. The 10S Pro+ adds a 7500mAh battery (against 7050mAh), 120W charging (against 80W), and RAM and storage options that start at 16GB and 512GB. The 10S Pro is lighter at 229 g (against 235 g) and has a lower listed price.
Does it support wireless charging?
No. The specification table lists wireless charging as not supported. Charging is wired, at up to 80W.
Can I expand the storage?
No. There is no memory card slot, so choose from the 256GB, 512GB or 1TB options when you buy.
Is it a good phone for photography?
The listed cameras are a 50MP main with OIS, a 50MP ultrawide and a 2MP macro, with no telephoto lens. The page positions the camera as secondary to gaming, so it is better suited to casual photos than to photography-led buying. Check independent samples if image quality is a priority.
Does it have a 3.5mm headphone jack or an IP rating?
Neither appears in this page's specification table. The USB-C 3.2 Gen 2 port handles charging and data. If wired audio or water resistance matters, confirm with the retailer or manufacturer before buying.
What does the Leading Version in the chipset name mean?
It is RedMagic's name for a higher-clocked variant of the Snapdragon 8 Elite, with the CPU listed on this page at up to 4.47GHz. The GPU is listed as an Adreno 830.
What are the listed price and release date?
SpecHub lists the RedMagic 10S Pro at $699 and a June 2025 release. Those are launch-period figures, and prices differ by region, memory configuration and retailer, so check the official store for current offers.
